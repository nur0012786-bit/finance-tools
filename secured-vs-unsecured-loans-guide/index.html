Secured vs. Unsecured Loans: What's the Difference?

Learn how secured and unsecured loans differ, how collateral affects borrowing, and what to compare before taking out a loan.

Educational finance guidance

A secured loan is generally backed by collateral, while an unsecured loan does not rely on a specific asset as security. The exact terms depend on the lender and loan agreement.

What is a secured loan?

A secured loan is backed by collateral that gives the lender a security interest in a specified asset. If the borrower does not meet the loan agreement, the lender may have rights concerning that collateral under applicable law and the contract.

What is an unsecured loan?

An unsecured loan is not backed by a specific asset. Lenders instead evaluate the applicant using factors such as credit history, income, existing obligations and other underwriting criteria.

How collateral can affect a loan

Collateral can affect eligibility, pricing and the lender's risk because a specified asset supports the debt. This does not mean every secured loan has a lower rate or every borrower will qualify.

Key differences to compare

Compare the interest rate, APR, loan amount, term, fees, collateral requirements, consequences of default and total repayment. Read the loan agreement for the actual rights and obligations.

Illustrative example

Imagine two hypothetical $10,000 loans with similar repayment terms. One is secured by an eligible asset and the other is unsecured. Their rates, fees and approval requirements can differ because the lender is taking different forms of risk. The actual comparison depends on the offers you receive.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

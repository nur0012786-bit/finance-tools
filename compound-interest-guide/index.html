Compound Interest Explained: How Your Money Can Grow

Learn how compound interest works, why time matters, and how rate, starting balance and regular contributions affect savings growth.

Educational finance guidance

Compound growth means interest or investment earnings can be added to the balance so that future growth can build on a larger base. The exact result depends on the account and compounding terms.

The basic idea

With compound interest, earnings can be added to the balance and then earn additional interest in later periods. This differs from simple interest, where interest is calculated only on the original principal under the simplified model.

The factors that matter

Starting balance, contribution amount, interest rate, compounding frequency and time all affect the mathematical result.

Why time matters

Because growth compounds on previous growth, a longer time horizon can create a larger difference even when the starting balance and rate remain the same.

Regular contributions

Adding money over time can increase the balance that participates in future compounding. Contribution timing can affect the result.

Illustrative example

A hypothetical $5,000 balance earning a constant annual rate can grow over several years without additional contributions. Adding regular contributions would produce a different result.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

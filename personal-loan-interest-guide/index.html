INTEREST BASICS

What is personal loan interest?

Interest is the cost charged for borrowing money. On an installment personal loan, the borrower typically makes scheduled payments that include amounts applied to interest and principal. Over time, the outstanding principal generally declines as payments are made according to the amortization schedule.

How the rate affects your payment

A higher interest rate generally increases the scheduled payment and total interest for the same loan amount and term. A lower rate can reduce those amounts, although actual savings depend on the full loan terms and fees.

How loan term changes interest

A longer repayment term can reduce the required monthly payment because the balance is spread across more payments. However, keeping a balance outstanding for more time can increase total interest paid. Comparing both the monthly payment and total repayment is therefore important.

Interest rate vs. APR

The interest rate measures the rate charged on the loan. APR can incorporate certain finance charges and is designed to help consumers compare borrowing costs. The exact APR calculation depends on the loan and applicable rules, so review the lender's disclosures for the actual figures.

Illustrative example

Imagine two hypothetical $10,000 personal loans with the same three-year term but different fixed interest rates. The loan with the higher rate would generally have a higher scheduled payment and more interest over the term, assuming comparable fee structures. This is why rate, term and fees should be evaluated together.

This example is educational only and does not represent a lender quote or a personalized borrowing recommendation.

Why the first payments can contain more interest

For an amortizing loan, interest is generally calculated from the outstanding balance according to the loan agreement. When the balance is largest at the beginning, the interest portion of a scheduled payment can be larger. As principal declines, the interest portion can decline as well.

What to check in a loan offer

  • Interest rate and whether it is fixed or variable
  • APR and applicable finance charges
  • Loan amount and repayment term
  • Scheduled monthly payment
  • Origination or other upfront fees
  • Total amount scheduled to be repaid
  • Late-payment terms and any applicable prepayment conditions

Frequently asked questions

Does a lower monthly payment always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer term can reduce the scheduled monthly payment while increasing the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Does APR include every possible cost?

APR is intended to reflect certain borrowing costs under applicable rules, but it should not be treated as a guarantee that every possible expense in every transaction is included. Review the lender's disclosures.

Can I calculate my exact interest cost with a calculator?

You can estimate it from the assumptions you enter, but actual lender calculations can vary because of fees, payment timing, rounding and the specific terms of the loan agreement.

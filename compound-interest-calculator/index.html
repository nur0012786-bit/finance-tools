What is compound interest?

Compound interest means you can earn returns on both your original money and the interest that has accumulated. Over longer periods, this compounding effect can become a significant part of your account growth.

For example, if you start with $10,000, contribute $300 each month and earn an average 7% annual return for 20 years, the account can grow substantially because contributions and accumulated returns continue working over time.

Why regular contributions matter

Adding money consistently can increase your future balance because each contribution gets more time to potentially earn returns. The earlier money is invested, the more opportunity it has to compound.

Compound interest formula

A basic compound interest calculation can be represented as A = P(1 + r/n)nt, where P is the principal, r is the annual rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the number of years. Regular contributions require an additional contribution calculation, so this calculator models monthly deposits separately.

How this US compound interest calculator works

This calculator estimates future value from an initial amount, recurring monthly contributions, an annual rate, a compounding frequency and an investment period. It separates the projected ending balance into your contributions and estimated growth.

Compounding means that previously earned interest or investment growth can itself contribute to future growth. The SEC describes compounding as earning on your savings and on the interest those savings have already earned.

Compound interest formula

For a single deposit with no additional contributions, a standard compound-growth model is A = P(1 + r/n)nt, where P is the starting principal, r is the annual rate expressed as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the number of years. Regular contributions require an additional contribution schedule, which this calculator models separately.

Worked example

For example, enter $10,000 as the initial investment, add $300 per month, use a 7% annual rate and choose a 10-year period. The calculator estimates the ending balance and shows how much came from deposits versus projected growth.

Compounding vs. investment returns

A calculator can illustrate mathematical growth, but an investment account does not necessarily earn a fixed or guaranteed rate. Actual returns can vary, and investment products can have fees, taxes and market risk. The SEC notes that investing involves risk and that fees can affect investment growth over time.

What this calculator does not model

This tool does not account for taxes, investment fees, inflation, withdrawals, changing returns, employer matches or account-specific rules. Treat the rate as an assumed average annual return for planning scenarios, not a guaranteed result.

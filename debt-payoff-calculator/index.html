How a debt payoff calculator works

The calculator applies monthly interest to the outstanding balance and then subtracts your monthly payment. It repeats this process until the balance reaches zero. An extra payment is added to your regular monthly payment to show its potential effect.

Why extra payments can reduce interest

Interest is often calculated from the amount of debt still outstanding. Paying additional principal can reduce the balance faster, which may reduce the amount of future interest charged when the interest rate and payment rules remain constant.

Debt payoff example

Suppose you have a $10,000 balance at an 18% annual interest rate and pay $300 per month. Adding another $100 per month can change both the payoff timeline and the total interest in the calculator's fixed-rate model.

DEBT PAYOFF GUIDE

How this US debt payoff calculator works

The calculator models a fixed-rate amortizing debt using the current balance, annual interest rate and planned monthly payment. Each simulated payment first accounts for monthly interest, with the remaining amount reducing principal. This continues until the balance reaches zero.

The model is intended for debts with a predictable interest rate and payment structure. Variable-rate loans, fees, penalties, irregular payments and payment rules that differ from this model can produce different results.

Why extra payments can reduce interest

When an additional payment reduces principal, future interest is calculated on a smaller balance. That can shorten the repayment period and reduce total interest. The CFPB explains that paying down principal faster can reduce interest costs, but borrowers should check how their servicer applies extra payments.

Debt payoff example

For example, enter a $10,000 balance at 12% APR with a $250 monthly payment. Then test an additional $50 monthly payment to compare the estimated payoff period and interest.

Payoff amount vs. account balance

The amount shown on an account statement may not always be identical to the exact amount required to satisfy a debt on a particular date. For loans such as mortgages, a lender or servicer may provide a formal payoff amount that accounts for interest through a specified date and possible fees.

Debt reduction methods

If you have several debts, common approaches include directing extra money toward the highest-interest debt or toward the smallest balance. The CFPB describes both approaches; this calculator models one debt at a time rather than comparing multiple debts simultaneously.

What does a debt payoff calculator show?

It estimates the number of monthly payments, total interest and total amount paid needed to eliminate a debt balance under the assumptions you enter.

Does an extra payment reduce interest?

An extra payment can reduce interest in a fixed-rate model because more money goes toward reducing the outstanding principal sooner.

What if my monthly payment is too low?

If the payment does not cover the first month's interest, the balance will not decline under the calculator's model. You should review the debt terms with your lender or card issuer.

Does this include late fees?

No. Late fees, annual fees, penalty rates and other charges are not included.

Can I use this for credit card debt?

Yes, as a simplified estimate when you know the balance, APR and intended monthly payment. Actual credit card rules can differ.

Can I use it for a personal loan?

Yes. For a fixed-rate personal loan, the calculator can provide a useful payoff estimate based on the entered balance, rate and payment.

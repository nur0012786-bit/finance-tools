Fixed vs. Variable Loan Rates: What Is the Difference?

Understand fixed and variable loan rates, how payment changes can work, and what to review before choosing a loan.

Educational finance guidance

A fixed-rate loan generally keeps its stated interest rate unchanged according to the loan terms. A variable-rate loan can change when its contract specifies an adjustment mechanism.

Fixed-rate loans

A fixed rate stays unchanged for the applicable period under the loan agreement. This can make scheduled payments easier to plan when other payment terms remain unchanged.

Variable-rate loans

A variable rate can adjust based on the terms of the loan. The agreement may describe an index, margin, adjustment schedule and caps or other limits.

Why payment changes matter

When a variable rate changes, the interest portion and potentially the required payment can change. Review the loan documents to understand when adjustments can occur and how large they can be.

What to compare

Compare the starting rate, APR, adjustment frequency, index, margin, caps, minimums, maximums, fees, loan term and projected payment under different scenarios.

Illustrative example

Suppose two hypothetical loans have the same starting balance. One has a fixed rate and the other starts lower but can adjust later. The variable-rate loan could have a different future payment even if both payments begin at the same amount.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

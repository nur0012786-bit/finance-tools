How a retirement calculator works

A retirement calculator estimates how a retirement account may grow when your existing savings and regular contributions are invested for a period of time. The result depends heavily on the return assumption, contribution amount and number of years.

Why time matters for retirement savings

Longer investment periods can give contributions and accumulated investment growth more time to compound. Starting earlier can therefore change the amount that needs to be contributed later, although investment returns are never guaranteed.

Example retirement projection

If you begin with $25,000, contribute $500 each month and assume a 7% annual return for 25 years, this calculator estimates the potential account value under those assumptions. A real portfolio can perform differently from year to year.

Frequently asked questions

US RETIREMENT PLANNING

How this retirement calculator works

This calculator projects a retirement account balance from your current savings, recurring monthly contributions, an assumed annual rate of return and the number of years until your target date. It separates the projected balance into your contributions and estimated investment growth.

Long-term investing can benefit from compound growth, but investment returns are not fixed or guaranteed. Investor.gov notes that investing involves risk and that markets fluctuate over time.

Why time and regular contributions matter

Starting earlier gives contributions more time to potentially compound. Regular contributions also add new money throughout the saving period. Investor.gov describes long-term retirement saving as a process built over working years and notes that workplace plans such as 401(k), 403(b) and 457(b) plans may provide tax advantages and employer matching.

Worked example

For example, enter $50,000 as current savings, contribute $500 per month, assume a 7% annual return and use a 20-year period. The calculator estimates the ending balance and shows the portion represented by your contributions versus projected growth.

Return assumptions are not guarantees

A projected annual return is an assumption for a planning scenario, not a promise of future performance. Actual returns can vary substantially from year to year, and fees, taxes, inflation, withdrawals and changes in contributions can materially affect retirement savings.

Retirement accounts and Social Security

This calculator is a general growth projection. It does not calculate IRA or 401(k) contribution limits, employer matching, income taxes, required minimum distributions or Social Security benefits. Investor.gov provides separate retirement and Social Security planning tools for those topics.

What does a retirement calculator estimate?

It estimates a possible future retirement balance using current savings, regular contributions, an assumed annual return and the number of years until retirement.

Are the investment returns guaranteed?

No. The annual return is only an assumption used for the projection. Actual investment performance can be higher or lower.

Does this calculator include inflation?

No. The displayed future balance is a nominal projection and does not adjust for inflation.

Does it include employer matching?

No. Employer contributions are not included. You can adjust your own monthly contribution to model a different total contribution assumption.

Does it account for taxes and fees?

No. Taxes and investment or account fees can reduce actual results and should be considered separately.

Can I use this for a 401(k) or IRA estimate?

Yes, as a general growth illustration. Account-specific contribution limits, tax rules, withdrawals and employer benefits are not modeled.

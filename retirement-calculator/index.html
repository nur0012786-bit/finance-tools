How a retirement calculator works

A retirement calculator estimates how a retirement account may grow when your existing savings and regular contributions are invested for a period of time. The result depends heavily on the return assumption, contribution amount and number of years.

Why time matters for retirement savings

Longer investment periods can give contributions and accumulated investment growth more time to compound. Starting earlier can therefore change the amount that needs to be contributed later, although investment returns are never guaranteed.

Example retirement projection

If you begin with $25,000, contribute $500 each month and assume a 7% annual return for 25 years, this calculator estimates the potential account value under those assumptions. A real portfolio can perform differently from year to year.

Frequently asked questions

What does a retirement calculator estimate?

It estimates a possible future retirement balance using current savings, regular contributions, an assumed annual return and the number of years until retirement.

Are the investment returns guaranteed?

No. The annual return is only an assumption used for the projection. Actual investment performance can be higher or lower.

Does this calculator include inflation?

No. The displayed future balance is a nominal projection and does not adjust for inflation.

Does it include employer matching?

No. Employer contributions are not included. You can adjust your own monthly contribution to model a different total contribution assumption.

Does it account for taxes and fees?

No. Taxes and investment or account fees can reduce actual results and should be considered separately.

Can I use this for a 401(k) or IRA estimate?

Yes, as a general growth illustration. Account-specific contribution limits, tax rules, withdrawals and employer benefits are not modeled.

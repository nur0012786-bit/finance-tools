HOW IT WORKS

How this closing costs calculator works

The calculator groups entered charges into three planning categories. Your lender's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure use their own detailed line-item classifications.

  • Loan costs: lender origination charges, discount points, appraisal and credit-report fees, and title or settlement services.
  • Government and other services: recording or transfer fees, other government charges, inspections, surveys and other services.
  • Prepaids and initial escrow: prepaid interest, homeowners insurance paid in advance, initial property-tax and insurance escrow deposits, and entered HOA or other prepaid items.

Total closing costs before credits are the sum of those categories. Lender credits reduce that amount. Costs paid before closing and costs financed from loan proceeds are then deducted from the remaining amount due at closing. Seller credits are applied only against the remaining costs in this model, not the down payment. Earnest money is then applied against the remaining borrower funds due.

The estimated new loan amount is the purchase price minus the down payment, plus costs you enter as financed. Estimated cash needed at closing is the down payment plus closing costs still due, less applied seller credits and earnest money. Any excess credit or deposit is shown separately rather than silently reducing the cash requirement below zero.

This model excludes property-tax proration, seller-paid individual line items, adjustments for expenses paid or unpaid by the seller, funds for the borrower, other third-party credits, loan-program financing restrictions and lender-specific treatment. Actual cash to close may differ.

US MORTGAGE GUIDANCE

Closing costs vs. cash to close

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau distinguishes total closing costs from cash to close. Closing costs are upfront loan and transaction expenses, excluding the down payment. Cash to close also accounts for the down payment, costs paid before closing, costs financed from the loan amount, the deposit, seller credits and other adjustments or credits.

For covered mortgage transactions, the lender generally provides a Loan Estimate after application and a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before scheduled closing. Review final figures against your most recent Loan Estimate and ask the lender or settlement agent about changes you do not understand.

TYPICAL RANGE

How much are closing costs?

There is no single national closing-cost amount. Freddie Mac says buyers should generally be prepared for closing costs of about 2% to 5% of the home's purchase price, while emphasizing that costs vary by location and transaction. See Freddie Mac's homebuying cost guide for the cost categories it discusses.

For example, on a $400,000 home, a 2% to 5% planning range would be $8,000 to $20,000. That range is only a budgeting reference; using actual estimates from your lender and service providers is more useful for a specific purchase.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Example home purchase

Suppose a home costs $400,000 and you plan to put $80,000 down. With the default cost inputs, loan costs total $5,700, government and other service costs total $2,300, and prepaids and initial escrow total $4,900. This produces $12,900 of estimated closing costs before credits. With no credits, no costs paid before closing or financed, and $5,000 of earnest money, estimated cash needed at closing is $87,900.

The estimated mortgage amount before any separate loan-program fees is $320,000. Your actual cash requirement can be different because your Loan Estimate, provider quotes, negotiated credits, prepaid items and transaction adjustments may differ.

FAQ

Closing costs calculator FAQs

What are closing costs?

Closing costs are upfront costs associated with getting a mortgage and completing a real estate transaction. They can include lender charges, appraisal and credit-report fees, title and settlement services, government charges, prepaid expenses and initial escrow deposits.

Are closing costs the same as cash to close?

No. Closing costs are one part of the amount you may need to bring to closing. Cash to close can also reflect the down payment, earnest money already paid, seller credits and other transaction adjustments.

Does this calculator include the down payment?

Yes, in estimated cash needed at closing. The down payment is shown separately and is not included in the total closing-cost figure.

What are lender credits and seller credits?

Lender credits are rebates from the lender that offset some closing costs and are commonly associated with a higher interest rate. Seller credits are amounts the seller has agreed to contribute toward eligible closing costs. Program rules and the purchase agreement can limit how credits are applied; this calculator caps them at estimated costs remaining in its simplified model.

What are prepaid costs and escrow deposits?

Prepaids can include interest from closing through the end of the month and an upfront homeowners insurance premium. Initial escrow deposits fund the starting balance for future property taxes, insurance and other charges where an escrow account is used. They are different from recurring monthly escrow payments.

Why do closing costs vary?

Costs vary with the loan type, lender, property location, services selected, taxes, insurance, timing and negotiated transaction terms. Some costs are also paid by the seller or offset by credits depending on the contract and applicable rules.

Is this an official Closing Disclosure?

No. This calculator is an educational estimate. Your lender's Loan Estimate and final Closing Disclosure contain the transaction-specific figures you should use for the actual purchase.

Can I enter costs paid before closing or financed in my loan?

Yes. Enter those figures separately. Costs paid before closing have already been paid and reduce the amount due at settlement. Costs financed from loan proceeds increase the estimated loan amount and reduce upfront cash due.

When should I check my final closing costs?

For covered mortgage transactions, the CFPB says you generally receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Review it against your most recent Loan Estimate and ask the lender about unexpected changes.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not provide a lender quote, Loan Estimate or Closing Disclosure. Closing costs and cash to close can change before closing. Review your official documents and ask your lender or settlement professional about any unexpected amount.

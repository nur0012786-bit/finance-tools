HOW IT WORKS

How the closing costs calculator works

This tool separates common mortgage and transaction expenses into two broad groups. Loan costs include lender charges, discount points and services commonly connected with obtaining the loan. Other costs can include government charges, prepaid expenses and initial escrow amounts.

The calculator subtracts lender credits from the estimated costs to produce estimated closing costs. It then combines the down payment and closing costs and subtracts seller credits and earnest money already paid to estimate cash to close.

These categories are designed to help you organize estimates. They are not a reproduction of every line on a Closing Disclosure.

US MORTGAGE GUIDANCE

Closing costs vs. cash to close

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that closing costs are upfront costs associated with getting the loan and transferring ownership. Cash to close is different: it is the amount you actually need to pay at closing after accounting for items such as the down payment, closing costs, deposits and credits.

For most covered mortgage transactions, the lender provides a Loan Estimate early in the process and a Closing Disclosure with final terms and costs. The CFPB says the Closing Disclosure must generally be provided at least three business days before closing.

FAQ

Closing costs calculator FAQs

What can be included in closing costs?

Depending on the transaction, costs can include lender charges, points, appraisal, title and settlement services, government fees, prepaid interest, insurance, initial escrow deposits and other transaction charges. The CFPB's Closing Disclosure groups costs into categories such as Loan Costs and Other Costs.

Are closing costs the same as cash to close?

No. Closing costs are one part of the amount you may need to bring to closing. Cash to close can also reflect the down payment, deposits already paid, seller credits and other adjustments.

What are lender credits?

Lender credits can offset some closing costs. They may be provided in exchange for a higher interest rate, so borrowers should consider the total cost of the loan.

Do I have to use these default amounts?

No. Replace every estimate with figures from your lender, title company, insurer, tax authority or other provider when available.

Why can closing costs vary so much?

Costs can differ because of the loan type, property location, lender, services, taxes, insurance, timing and negotiated transaction terms.

Is this calculator an official Closing Disclosure?

No. Your lender's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure are the transaction-specific documents to review. This calculator is for educational planning only.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not provide a lender quote, Loan Estimate or Closing Disclosure. Closing costs and cash to close can change before closing. Review your official documents and ask your lender or settlement professional about any unexpected amount.

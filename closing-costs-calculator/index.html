HOW IT WORKS

How this closing costs calculator works

The calculator organizes your inputs into loan costs and other transaction costs. Loan costs include lender charges, discount points, appraisal and credit-report expenses, and title or settlement costs entered in the form. Other costs can include government charges, inspections or other services, prepaid interest, homeowners insurance, initial escrow deposits and other prepaid amounts.

Estimated closing costs are calculated as the total entered costs minus lender credits, with the result prevented from going below zero. Estimated cash to close is then calculated from the down payment plus estimated closing costs, less seller credits and earnest money already paid.

This is an organizing and planning model, not a replica of every line on a Loan Estimate or Closing Disclosure. Some charges may be categorized differently on your lender's documents, and transaction-specific adjustments can change the final amount.

US MORTGAGE GUIDANCE

Closing costs vs. cash to close

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes closing costs as upfront costs associated with obtaining the mortgage and transferring ownership. Cash to close is a separate figure: it reflects the amount you need to pay at closing after the transaction's down payment, closing costs, deposits, credits and other adjustments are taken into account.

For covered mortgage transactions, a lender generally provides a Loan Estimate after application and a Closing Disclosure with final loan terms and costs. The CFPB says the Closing Disclosure must generally be provided at least three business days before closing, giving you time to compare the final figures with your Loan Estimate.

TYPICAL RANGE

How much are closing costs?

There is no single national closing-cost amount. Freddie Mac says buyers should generally be prepared for closing costs of about 2% to 5% of the home's purchase price, while emphasizing that costs vary by location and transaction.

For example, on a $400,000 home, a 2% to 5% planning range would be $8,000 to $20,000. That range is only a budgeting reference; using actual estimates from your lender and service providers is more useful for a specific purchase.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Example home purchase

Suppose a home costs $400,000 and you plan to put $80,000 down. Using the calculator's default inputs, loan costs total $5,700 and other entered costs total $7,200, producing $12,900 of estimated closing costs before credits. With $5,000 of earnest money already paid and no seller or lender credits, estimated cash to close is $87,900.

The estimated mortgage amount before any separate loan-program fees is $320,000. Your actual cash requirement can be different because your Loan Estimate, provider quotes, negotiated credits, prepaid items and transaction adjustments may differ.

FAQ

Closing costs calculator FAQs

What are closing costs?

Closing costs are upfront costs associated with getting a mortgage and completing a real estate transaction. They can include lender charges, appraisal and credit-report fees, title and settlement services, government charges, prepaid expenses and initial escrow deposits.

Are closing costs the same as cash to close?

No. Closing costs are one part of the amount you may need to bring to closing. Cash to close can also reflect the down payment, earnest money already paid, seller credits and other transaction adjustments.

Does this calculator include the down payment?

The calculator shows the down payment separately and uses it when estimating cash to close. It does not treat the down payment as part of the displayed estimated closing-cost figure.

What are lender credits?

Lender credits can offset some closing costs. The CFPB notes that a lender may provide a credit in exchange for a higher interest rate or through other loan pricing, so compare the overall cost rather than looking only at the upfront credit.

What are prepaid costs and escrow deposits?

Prepaid costs can include items such as prepaid interest and homeowners insurance. Initial escrow deposits can fund an account used for future property taxes, insurance and other applicable charges.

Why do closing costs vary?

Costs vary with the loan type, lender, property location, services selected, taxes, insurance, timing and negotiated transaction terms. Some costs are also paid by the seller or offset by credits depending on the contract and applicable rules.

Is this an official Closing Disclosure?

No. This calculator is an educational estimate. Your lender's Loan Estimate and final Closing Disclosure contain the transaction-specific figures you should use for the actual purchase.

When should I check my final closing costs?

For covered mortgage transactions, the CFPB says you generally receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Review it against your most recent Loan Estimate and ask the lender about unexpected changes.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not provide a lender quote, Loan Estimate or Closing Disclosure. Closing costs and cash to close can change before closing. Review your official documents and ask your lender or settlement professional about any unexpected amount.

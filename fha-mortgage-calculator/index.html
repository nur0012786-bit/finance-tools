HOW IT WORKS

How this US FHA mortgage calculator works

The calculator starts with the purchase price and subtracts your down payment to estimate the base FHA loan. It then calculates upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP) from the base loan amount. In this model, UFMIP is assumed to be financed, so it is added to the base loan before principal and interest are calculated.

Monthly MIP is estimated by taking the financed starting loan balance, multiplying it by the annual MIP rate you enter, and dividing by 12. This is a simplified planning method; actual FHA mortgage-insurance billing depends on the applicable FHA premium structure and loan balance. HUD publishes the applicable MIP rules and rates.

The calculator then adds estimated monthly property tax and homeowners insurance to principal, interest, and MIP. It does not include closing costs, HOA dues, maintenance, utilities, discount points, lender-specific fees, or other transaction costs.

FHA down payment and UFMIP

HUD states that FHA 203(b) financing can provide approximately 96.5% financing for eligible borrowers, and the upfront mortgage insurance premium may be financed into the mortgage. That corresponds to a commonly used 3.5% minimum down-payment scenario, subject to eligibility and underwriting requirements.

2026 FHA loan limits

FHA loan limits vary by county or metropolitan area and property size. For 2026, the national one-unit floor is $541,287 and the national one-unit ceiling is $1,249,125. These figures are not a single nationwide borrowing limit: the applicable limit depends on the property's location and number of units.

If your calculated base loan exceeds the applicable local FHA limit, the result should not be treated as an FHA-eligible loan amount. Verify the current limit for the property location before relying on the estimate.

EXAMPLE

Example FHA payment

Suppose you purchase a $350,000 home with a 3.5% down payment, a 6.25% interest rate, a 30-year term, 1.75% UFMIP, 0.55% annual MIP, 1.1% annual property tax, and $1,800 of annual homeowners insurance. The 3.5% down payment is $12,250, producing a $337,750 base loan. At 1.75%, the estimated UFMIP is about $5,911, giving a starting financed loan of about $343,661.

Using those inputs, the calculator estimates principal and interest from the financed loan and then adds monthly MIP, property tax, and homeowners insurance. Your actual FHA payment can differ because the applicable MIP structure, loan limit, lender terms, property taxes, insurance, and other costs can vary.

FAQ

FHA mortgage calculator FAQs

What does an FHA mortgage calculator estimate?

It estimates the base FHA loan, upfront MIP, financed starting loan, principal and interest, monthly MIP, property tax, homeowners insurance, and a combined monthly payment based on your inputs.

How much down payment is required for FHA financing?

HUD states that eligible FHA 203(b) borrowers can receive approximately 96.5% financing, which corresponds to about 3.5% down. Eligibility, credit requirements, and lender underwriting still apply.

What is FHA UFMIP?

UFMIP is the upfront mortgage insurance premium required for most FHA single-family mortgage-insurance programs. HUD's current published structure lists 1.75% of the base loan amount for standard purchase/refinance cases, subject to program-specific exceptions.

What is annual FHA MIP?

Annual MIP is an ongoing mortgage-insurance premium generally collected in monthly installments. The applicable rate depends on factors such as loan term, base loan amount, and loan-to-value, so the calculator lets you enter the rate used for your scenario.

Are FHA loan limits the same everywhere?

No. FHA forward loan limits vary by county or metropolitan area and property size. For 2026, the one-unit national floor is $541,287 and ceiling is $1,249,125, but a property's applicable local limit may fall between those figures.

Does this calculate FHA approval?

No. This is a payment and insurance estimate. FHA eligibility and approval depend on borrower, property, documentation, lender, and program requirements.

Does this include closing costs?

No. The calculator does not include closing costs, lender fees, discount points, HOA dues, maintenance, utilities, or other transaction-specific expenses.

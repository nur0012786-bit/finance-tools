Debt Payoff Methods Explained: Avalanche vs. Snowball

Learn how common debt payoff methods work, including the debt avalanche and debt snowball, and how to organize a repayment plan.

Educational finance guidance

Debt payoff methods are budgeting strategies for deciding how to direct extra repayment money across multiple debts. They do not change the contractual minimum payments you owe.

Debt avalanche

The avalanche approach directs extra money toward the debt with the highest interest rate after required minimum payments are covered. When that balance is paid, the extra amount can move to the next target.

Debt snowball

The snowball approach directs extra money toward the smallest balance after required minimum payments are covered. Paying off smaller balances first can simplify the number of active debts.

Why minimum payments still matter

Required minimum payments are contractual obligations. A payoff strategy generally starts by making required payments on all accounts and then directing available extra money according to the selected method.

Interest and time

Interest rates, balances, payment timing and new charges affect how quickly debt is repaid and the total interest paid.

Illustrative example

A borrower with three balances can compare the two methods by keeping minimum payments constant and changing which balance receives the extra amount. The resulting payoff timeline and interest depend on the actual account terms.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

HOW IT WORKS

How this US HELOC payment calculator works

A home equity line of credit is a revolving line secured by your home. Unlike a home equity loan, a HELOC can generally allow repeated borrowing up to a credit limit during a draw period. After the draw period, the account normally enters a repayment period under the terms of the plan.

For an interest-only estimate, this calculator multiplies the entered balance by the annual rate and divides by 12. That produces a simple monthly interest estimate and does not reduce principal.

For a principal-and-interest estimate, the calculator uses the entered balance, rate and repayment period in a standard fixed-rate amortization formula. This is a planning model; the actual HELOC agreement controls how minimum payments are calculated.

Draw period vs. repayment period

During the draw period, you may generally be able to borrow from available credit and make payments under the plan's rules. When the draw period ends, additional borrowing may stop and repayment terms can apply. The repayment payment can be substantially higher than an earlier interest-only payment because principal must also be repaid.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau notes that HELOCs usually have variable rates, so payments can change as the rate changes. Some plans may also provide a fixed-rate conversion option for part or all of a balance.

Worked example

Suppose the outstanding HELOC balance is $50,000 and the entered annual rate is 8.5%. A simple interest-only estimate is about $354 per month ($50,000 × 8.5% ÷ 12). If the same $50,000 balance were amortized over a 20-year repayment period at a constant 8.5% rate, the principal-and-interest estimate would be higher because the payment also repays principal.

The example assumes the full $50,000 balance remains unchanged. Real HELOC balances can rise or fall as you draw funds and repay principal.

WHAT CAN CHANGE THE PAYMENT?

Why an actual HELOC payment can differ

Your actual payment depends on the HELOC contract. Important variables can include the outstanding balance, index and margin used for a variable rate, rate caps, minimum-payment formula, draw activity, repayment term and lender fees.

A HELOC may also have application, appraisal, annual, closing or other charges. Those costs are not included in the calculator's payment estimate.

Because a HELOC is secured by the home, failing to repay amounts owed can put the property at risk. Treat the calculator as a cost-estimation tool rather than an approval or borrowing recommendation.

HELOC VS. HOME EQUITY LOAN

HELOC compared with a home equity loan

A HELOC is generally a revolving line that can be drawn multiple times within the available limit. A home equity loan generally provides a specific lump sum and is repaid under a set schedule. A HELOC commonly has an adjustable rate, while a home equity loan may use a fixed or adjustable rate depending on the product.

Both can be secured by the home, so the repayment terms and consequences of missed payments should be reviewed carefully.

FAQ

US HELOC payment calculator FAQs

What is a HELOC?

A HELOC is a home equity line of credit secured by a home. It generally lets a borrower draw funds up to a credit limit and repay amounts under the terms of the plan.

How is an interest-only HELOC payment estimated?

This calculator multiplies the current balance by the entered annual rate and divides by 12. It is a simplified estimate and assumes the balance and rate stay unchanged.

Are HELOC rates fixed?

Many HELOCs use variable interest rates, so the payment can change as the rate changes. This calculator holds the entered rate constant.

What happens when the draw period ends?

Depending on the plan, additional borrowing may stop and the outstanding balance may enter a repayment period. Payments can increase when principal repayment begins.

Does a HELOC payment include principal?

It depends on the payment terms. Some plans can have interest-only payments during part of the draw period, while repayment terms can require principal and interest.

Does this calculator include HELOC fees?

No. It estimates payment and interest from the entered balance, rate and term. Application, appraisal, annual, closing and other lender-specific fees are excluded.

Can a HELOC rate change?

Yes. Many HELOCs have variable rates. A lender may use an index plus a margin and apply contractual rate limits. This calculator does not project future rate changes.

Is this a HELOC approval calculator?

No. It estimates payments only. Actual credit limits and eligibility depend on the lender, property, income, debt, credit profile and other underwriting factors.

Is a HELOC different from a home equity loan?

Yes. A HELOC is generally revolving credit, while a home equity loan generally provides a lump sum. Their rates, payment structures and terms can differ.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not determine eligibility, available credit or the contractual payment on a specific HELOC. Review the lender's disclosures for the actual rate, payment formula, fees, draw period and repayment terms.

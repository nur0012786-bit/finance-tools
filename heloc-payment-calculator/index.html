HELOC PAYMENT METHOD

How this HELOC payment calculator works

A home equity line of credit (HELOC) is revolving credit secured by your home. During the draw period, you may generally borrow against available credit, subject to the plan's terms. When the draw period ends, additional draws usually stop and the outstanding amount enters the repayment phase. The lender's agreement determines the actual minimum payment in each phase. Read the CFPB's HELOC overview.

1. Interest-only payment during the draw period

The calculator shows a common interest-only illustration:

Monthly draw-period interest = current balance × annual rate ÷ 12

This assumes the drawn balance stays unchanged, no additional money is borrowed, and no principal is paid down during the remaining draw period. Some HELOC plans require a principal component even during the draw phase, so this result is not a universal minimum-payment formula.

2. Fully amortizing payment during repayment

For the repayment illustration, the calculator assumes the balance at the end of the draw period is repaid in equal monthly principal-and-interest payments over the repayment period. It uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula with the entered rate held constant. It calculates repayment-phase interest from the total scheduled payments less the principal balance.

Under the interest-only draw assumption, the balance entering repayment is the same as the current balance. If your plan requires principal during the draw period, the actual balance at the phase transition may be lower. Some HELOC plans use a different minimum-payment formula or require the entire outstanding amount in a balloon payment instead of a fully amortizing schedule.

3. Total modeled interest

The calculator adds estimated interest during the remaining interest-only draw phase to the interest expected during the fully amortizing repayment phase. This is a scenario calculation, not a lender disclosure or a prediction of the actual lifetime cost. It assumes one current balance, no further draws, no principal paydown during draw, and an unchanged interest rate.

4. Rate-increase stress test

Many HELOCs have variable rates. A variable rate commonly has two parts: an index and a margin. The index can change with market conditions, while the margin is added under the credit agreement. The optional rate-increase test applies the number of percentage points you enter to the current rate and recalculates both phase estimates using that higher rate. It does not model the timing of index changes, introductory rates, periodic caps, lifetime caps, or a lender's exact payment recalculation method. Read the CFPB's guide to HELOC variable rates.

DRAW PERIOD VS. REPAYMENT

Why a HELOC payment can rise when the draw period ends

During the draw period, a plan may allow interest-only payments, or it may require a payment that includes some principal. When repayment begins, the borrower can no longer draw on the line and must repay the outstanding balance under the contract. Because principal must then be repaid over a shorter remaining period, the payment may rise substantially.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau notes that repayment often occurs over ten or 20 years, but some HELOC agreements may require the entire balance when the repayment period begins. A balloon payment can be large and may create refinancing or repayment risk. Review the CFPB's explanation of the repayment period.

The repayment payment shown by this calculator is a fully amortizing example. It is not a substitute for the payment formula in your HELOC agreement. Check whether your plan uses an amortization schedule, a percentage of the balance plus interest, a fixed payment, or a balloon amount.

VARIABLE RATE AND PAYMENT RISK

How a changing HELOC rate affects your payment

Many HELOCs use a variable rate based on an index plus a margin. Ask the lender which index applies, how often the rate can change, whether an introductory rate applies, and what periodic and lifetime rate caps are in the contract. A low introductory rate may increase when its introductory period ends.

The calculator's baseline keeps the current rate constant for both phases. Use the rate-increase test to see how a selected increase would affect an interest-only draw payment and a fully amortizing repayment payment. This is a simple sensitivity check; it is not a model of future rate paths or the exact effect of any contractual cap.

Because a HELOC is secured by your home, failure to make required payments can put the property at risk. Do not rely on a low draw-period interest-only estimate as evidence that the repayment-period payment will be affordable.

HELOC FEES AND CONTRACT TERMS

Costs this HELOC calculator does not include

HELOCs can involve application, origination, appraisal, title, closing, annual, inactivity, cancellation or rate-conversion fees. The fee types and amounts depend on the lender and agreement. This calculator estimates interest and principal payments only; it does not add these charges. See the CFPB's list of possible HELOC fees.

Before opening or using a HELOC, review the account-opening disclosures for the draw period, repayment period, minimum-payment formula, variable-rate index and margin, caps, fees, conversion options and any balloon-payment terms. Regulation Z requires creditors to disclose the relevant payment terms and explain when minimum payments may not repay the full balance. Read Regulation Z § 1026.40.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

HELOC payment calculator FAQs

What is a HELOC?

A home equity line of credit is revolving credit secured by your home. During the draw period, you may generally borrow up to the available credit limit, subject to the agreement.

How is an interest-only HELOC payment estimated?

This calculator multiplies the current drawn balance by the annual rate and divides by 12. It assumes a constant balance and rate for the remaining draw period. Some plans require principal as well as interest during draw, so check your agreement's minimum-payment formula.

What happens when the draw period ends?

Further borrowing usually stops and the outstanding balance enters the repayment period. Payments can rise because principal must be repaid. Some plans amortize the balance over a set period; others may require a balloon payment.

Does this calculator show the exact contractual minimum payment?

No. It shows an interest-only draw illustration and a fully amortizing repayment illustration. Actual minimum payments can be based on a percentage of the balance, principal plus interest, another formula, or a balloon payment under the agreement.

Are HELOC interest rates fixed?

Many HELOCs have variable rates, commonly based on an index plus a margin. The rate can change and affect the payment. Some plans allow a portion of the balance to be converted to a fixed rate.

What does the rate-increase test show?

It recalculates the draw-period interest-only payment and repayment-period principal-and-interest payment using a rate that is higher than the current input by the selected number of percentage points. It is a sensitivity scenario, not a forecast.

Does the calculator include new draws or principal payments during the draw period?

No. It assumes the current balance remains unchanged during the draw phase. Additional borrowing or principal repayment would change the balance entering repayment and the interest total.

Does the calculator include HELOC fees?

No. Application, appraisal, origination, closing, annual, inactivity, cancellation and conversion fees are excluded. Check the lender's disclosures for fees that apply to your account.

Can the lender require a balloon payment?

Some HELOC agreements may require the outstanding balance to be paid at the end of the draw period or at another contractual point. This calculator assumes a fully amortizing repayment phase and does not calculate a balloon amount.

Is a HELOC payment secured by my home?

Yes. A HELOC is generally secured by the home. If you cannot repay as agreed, the lender may have rights against the property, including foreclosure under applicable law.

HELOC VS. HOME EQUITY LOAN

HELOC compared with a home equity loan

A HELOC is generally a revolving line that can be drawn multiple times within the available limit. A home equity loan generally provides a specific lump sum and is repaid under a set schedule. A HELOC commonly has an adjustable rate, while a home equity loan may use a fixed or adjustable rate depending on the product.

Both can be secured by the home, so the repayment terms and consequences of missed payments should be reviewed carefully.

FAQ

US HELOC payment calculator FAQs

What is a HELOC?

A HELOC is a home equity line of credit secured by a home. It generally lets a borrower draw funds up to a credit limit and repay amounts under the terms of the plan.

How is an interest-only HELOC payment estimated?

This calculator multiplies the current balance by the entered annual rate and divides by 12. It is a simplified estimate and assumes the balance and rate stay unchanged.

Are HELOC rates fixed?

Many HELOCs use variable interest rates, so the payment can change as the rate changes. This calculator holds the entered rate constant.

What happens when the draw period ends?

Depending on the plan, additional borrowing may stop and the outstanding balance may enter a repayment period. Payments can increase when principal repayment begins.

Does a HELOC payment include principal?

It depends on the payment terms. Some plans can have interest-only payments during part of the draw period, while repayment terms can require principal and interest.

Does this calculator include HELOC fees?

No. It estimates payment and interest from the entered balance, rate and term. Application, appraisal, annual, closing and other lender-specific fees are excluded.

Can a HELOC rate change?

Yes. Many HELOCs have variable rates. A lender may use an index plus a margin and apply contractual rate limits. This calculator does not project future rate changes.

Is this a HELOC approval calculator?

No. It estimates payments only. Actual credit limits and eligibility depend on the lender, property, income, debt, credit profile and other underwriting factors.

Is a HELOC different from a home equity loan?

Yes. A HELOC is generally revolving credit, while a home equity loan generally provides a lump sum. Their rates, payment structures and terms can differ.

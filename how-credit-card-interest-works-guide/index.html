Educational finance guidance

Credit card interest depends on the card's agreement and billing practices. Unlike a typical fixed installment loan, a revolving balance can change as purchases, payments and credits occur.

APR and periodic rates

Credit card agreements generally disclose an APR and the way it is converted or applied for interest calculations. The exact method depends on the account terms.

Daily balances and billing cycles

Many credit card agreements use a daily balance methodology or another disclosed method. The statement and agreement explain how interest is determined.

Grace periods

Some cards provide a grace period for certain purchases when account conditions are met. Cash advances and other transaction types can have different rules.

Payments reduce balances

Payments generally reduce outstanding balances according to the card agreement and applicable payment-allocation rules. New purchases can increase the balance again.

Illustrative example

A cardholder with a revolving balance will generally incur more interest when the balance remains higher for longer, all else equal. Paying earlier or more can reduce the balance on which future interest is charged.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

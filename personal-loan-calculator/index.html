How to Use This Personal Loan Calculator

Use this personal loan payment calculator to estimate the monthly principal-and-interest payment on a fixed-rate installment loan. Enter the amount you plan to borrow, the annual interest rate and the repayment term. The results show the scheduled monthly payment, estimated total interest and total of payments over the selected term.

You can also enter an extra monthly payment to model an accelerated payoff. The comparison assumes the extra amount is applied to principal each month. Your lender's payment-allocation rules and loan agreement may produce different results.

Personal Loan Payment Formula

For a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments, the standard amortization formula is:

Monthly payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

  • P is the amount borrowed, or principal.
  • r is the monthly interest rate, calculated here as the annual interest rate divided by 12 and then by 100.
  • n is the total number of monthly payments.

When the interest rate is 0%, the estimated payment is the loan amount divided by the number of monthly payments. Each regular payment includes interest on the outstanding balance and a portion that reduces principal. As the balance falls, the interest portion generally decreases.

Personal Loan Interest Rate vs. APR

The interest rate is the cost charged for borrowing the principal. The annual percentage rate (APR) is a broader measure that can include the interest rate and certain loan fees. An offer with a lower advertised interest rate is not automatically the less expensive option if it has higher fees.

This calculator asks for the annual interest rate and models principal and interest. It does not calculate APR or add origination fees and other charges. When comparing offers, use the APR and the lender's written disclosures alongside the monthly payment and total amount you expect to repay. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains the difference between an interest rate and APR.

How the Loan Term Affects Your Payment and Interest

A longer repayment term usually lowers the required monthly payment for the same principal and interest rate, but it can increase the total interest paid because the balance is outstanding for more months. A shorter term usually requires a higher monthly payment but can reduce total interest.

Compare terms using a monthly payment that fits your budget and the full repayment cost—not the monthly payment alone. A payment that looks manageable can still make a loan expensive when fees and a longer term are included.

How Extra Monthly Payments Can Reduce Interest

On a typical amortizing loan, interest is calculated on the remaining balance. An additional payment applied to principal can reduce that balance sooner, which may reduce future interest and shorten the payoff period.

Use the extra monthly payment field to compare an illustrative accelerated schedule with the standard schedule. The model assumes the extra amount is paid every month and applied to principal. Check your agreement for prepayment terms, fees, payment timing and instructions for making principal-only payments. Your lender may apply payments differently.

How to Compare Personal Loan Offers

When reviewing personal loan offers, compare the same loan amount and repayment term so that the differences are meaningful. Review more than the advertised rate:

  • APR and the interest rate, including whether the rate is fixed or variable.
  • Origination fees and any other charges deducted from the amount you receive or added to what you owe.
  • Monthly payment, total repayment and the total interest over the expected term.
  • Late-payment fees, optional products and any conditions attached to the offer.
  • Whether additional payments or early payoff are allowed and whether charges apply.

The CFPB recommends reviewing the loan disclosures and checking for fees such as origination or documentation charges. Read its guidance on fees on personal installment loans before accepting an offer. Compare written terms from more than one lender rather than choosing based only on a headline rate.

What This Calculator Does Not Include

This tool estimates principal and interest for a standard fixed-rate installment loan with regular monthly payments. It does not include origination or application fees, documentation charges, optional credit or payment protection products, late fees, taxes, or other lender-specific costs. It also does not determine eligibility, predict an approval decision or quote a lender's rate.

Actual loan costs depend on the contract, lender, borrower and applicable state rules. Review the written loan agreement and required disclosures before accepting credit. Be cautious of anyone who guarantees approval in exchange for an upfront fee; the Federal Trade Commission explains warning signs of advance-fee loan scams.

Personal Loan Calculator FAQs

How much will my personal loan payment be?

Your estimated payment depends on the amount borrowed, annual interest rate and repayment term. Enter those values above to calculate a modeled monthly principal-and-interest payment.

How do I calculate interest on a personal loan?

For a typical fixed-rate installment loan, the monthly interest is calculated from the outstanding principal balance and the monthly rate. The rest of the payment reduces principal. This calculator models that monthly amortization process and adds the estimated interest over the schedule.

Is the interest rate the same as APR?

No. The interest rate measures the cost of borrowing, while APR can include the interest rate plus certain fees. This calculator uses the annual interest rate as its input and does not calculate APR or add lender fees.

Does a longer personal loan term cost more?

A longer term generally lowers the scheduled monthly payment but can increase total interest because repayment takes longer. Compare both the payment and the total repayment amount.

Can extra payments pay off a personal loan faster?

They can when the lender applies the extra amount to principal and the loan terms allow it. This calculator models an extra monthly principal payment; confirm payment instructions and any prepayment terms with your lender.

Does this calculator include origination fees?

No. It estimates principal and interest only. Origination, documentation, late-payment and optional-product charges can change the actual cost of borrowing.

Are these results a loan offer or approval estimate?

No. The results are educational estimates based on the values entered and the calculator's assumptions. Only a lender can provide the terms and disclosures for an actual loan offer.

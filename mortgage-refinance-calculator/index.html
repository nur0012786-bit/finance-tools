How this mortgage refinance calculator works

Refinancing replaces an existing mortgage with a new mortgage that pays off the old loan. This calculator keeps the current balance as the principal of the new loan and compares two fixed-rate principal-and-interest schedules.

Current payment: calculated from the current balance, current annual rate and remaining term. New payment: calculated from the same balance, new rate and new term.

Simple break-even: estimated refinance closing costs ÷ monthly principal-and-interest savings. If the new payment is not lower, this calculator reports that the simple break-even is not reached.

Interest comparison: remaining interest on the current loan is compared with interest over the full new loan term. Closing costs are then subtracted from the interest difference for the displayed after-cost figure.

Why the loan term matters

A refinance can lower the monthly payment partly because the new loan has a longer repayment period. For example, replacing a mortgage with 20 years remaining with a new 30-year mortgage can reduce the payment while extending the time the balance is outstanding. CFPB guidance specifically notes that borrowers should understand how much of a payment reduction comes from a lower rate versus a longer term. citeturn0search2

Refinance closing costs

Refinancing generally involves closing costs and fees. Freddie Mac says refinance costs vary by lender, credit profile and location and may include items such as origination, appraisal, credit report, title and government recording costs. Its current consumer guidance gives a broad planning range of about 3%–6% of loan principal, but your actual transaction can be outside that range. citeturn0search1

A “no-cost” refinance does not necessarily mean the transaction has no economic cost. CFPB explains that a lender may cover costs through a higher interest rate or by adding costs to the loan amount. citeturn0search0

Worked example

Using the default inputs, a borrower has a $300,000 balance at 7% with 25 years remaining and considers a new 25-year loan at 6%, with $6,000 of estimated refinance costs. The calculator estimates both monthly principal-and-interest payments, compares the remaining interest on each schedule, and divides the $6,000 cost by the monthly payment reduction to produce a simple break-even estimate.

This example is intentionally a calculation illustration rather than a recommendation. Actual refinance pricing, fees, eligibility and the borrower's expected time in the home can materially change the comparison.

What to compare with a lender

Use the calculator as a screening tool, then compare the actual Loan Estimate and closing documents. CFPB guidance recommends reviewing the interest rate, monthly payment, upfront loan costs, lender credits and cash to close when comparing mortgage offers. citeturn0search15

US mortgage refinance FAQs

What is mortgage refinancing?

Refinancing replaces an existing mortgage with a new loan. Common goals include changing the interest rate, monthly payment, repayment term or other loan features. citeturn0search2

How is the refinance break-even point calculated?

This calculator divides estimated closing costs by the monthly principal-and-interest payment reduction. It is a simplified calculation and does not capture every cost or benefit.

Can refinancing lower my monthly payment?

It can, depending on the new rate, loan term and balance. A longer new term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the total number of years you make payments.

Does refinancing always save money?

No. The comparison depends on the new rate and term, remaining balance, refinance costs and how long the new mortgage remains in place. A lower payment by itself does not establish an overall saving.

What refinance costs should I enter?

Use your best estimate of the costs you expect to pay for the new mortgage. Actual costs can include lender charges, appraisal, credit, title, government and other transaction-specific fees.

What if the lender gives me credits or rolls costs into the new loan?

Those structures change the economics of the refinance. Lender credits can reduce upfront costs while being associated with a higher rate, while financing costs increases the new loan balance. CFPB describes these tradeoffs in its mortgage guidance. citeturn0search4turn0search0

Does this calculator include taxes, insurance or mortgage insurance?

No. It compares principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and escrow deposits can change separately from the loan's principal-and-interest payment.

Does this calculator evaluate cash-out refinancing?

No. It assumes the current balance is the principal of the new loan. A cash-out refinance can have a larger loan balance and different payment, costs and objectives; use the Cash-Out Refinance Calculator for that scenario.

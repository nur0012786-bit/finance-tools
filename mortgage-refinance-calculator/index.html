How this mortgage refinance calculator works

This calculator compares keeping your current fixed-rate mortgage with replacing it using a new fixed-rate mortgage. It assumes the new loan starts with the current mortgage payoff amount. You can choose whether refinance closing costs are paid in cash at closing or added to the new loan balance.

What the calculator compares

  • Monthly principal and interest: the scheduled payment on the current loan versus the new loan.
  • Economic break-even: the first modeled month when the refinance scenario's cumulative payments, remaining payoff balance and any cash-paid closing costs are no greater than the keep-current-loan scenario.
  • Holding-period cost: at the number of years you enter, the calculator compares payments made plus the remaining loan payoff balance. It also includes closing costs paid in cash, or costs added to the financed balance.
  • Full-term net saving: remaining interest on the current loan minus interest on the new loan and refinance costs. This compares the full modeled schedules and is not the same as a short-term holding-period result.

The holding-period comparison is a nominal-dollar estimate. It does not discount future dollars, estimate tax effects or include home-sale costs. It makes the effect of the remaining mortgage balance visible rather than relying only on a lower monthly payment.

Why a lower payment may not mean lower cost

A new loan can reduce the monthly payment because its interest rate is lower, because its repayment term is longer, or both. Extending the term can reduce the monthly obligation while increasing the time you pay interest. Compare the remaining balance and total cost over the period you expect to keep the loan, not just the payment amount.

Worked example using the default inputs

Assume a current mortgage payoff of $300,000 at 7% with 25 years remaining. The proposed refinance is 6% for 25 years, with $6,000 in closing costs paid at closing.

  • Estimated current monthly principal and interest: $2,120.34.
  • Estimated new monthly principal and interest: $1,932.90.
  • Monthly principal-and-interest reduction: about $187.43.
  • Simple payment-only payback of $6,000: about 32.0 months.

The calculator's economic break-even and five-year holding-period comparison also account for how quickly each loan balance declines. Those results can differ from the simple payment-only calculation.

Closing costs: pay upfront or finance them

If costs are paid at closing, the new loan amount remains equal to the entered current payoff, but the costs are included as cash paid in the refinance comparison. If costs are financed, they increase the new principal, monthly payment and interest. A “no closing cost” offer can involve a higher interest rate or lender credits; compare actual Loan Estimates rather than assuming the costs disappear.

Compare real loan offers

The CFPB recommends comparing loan amount, interest rate, monthly principal and interest, mortgage insurance, total monthly payment, upfront costs, lender credits and cash to close. Its Loan Estimate comparison guide also suggests examining the five-year cost of borrowing, which helps reveal costs hidden by a lower monthly payment.

Important limitations

This is a planning estimate, not a lender quote or approval decision. It assumes fixed-rate, fully amortizing loans with monthly payments, and it assumes the current mortgage payoff is the starting principal for both scenarios. It does not model adjustable rates, cash-out proceeds, points separately from the costs entered, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, escrow, prepayment penalties, tax deductions or the time value of money. Verify transaction-specific figures using your lender's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure.

US mortgage refinance FAQs

What is mortgage refinancing?

Refinancing replaces an existing mortgage with a new loan. Borrowers may refinance to change the interest rate, payment, repayment term or other loan features.

How is refinance break-even calculated here?

This calculator finds the first modeled month when the refinance alternative's payments made, remaining mortgage payoff and any cash-paid closing costs are no greater than keeping the current mortgage. This is more complete than dividing costs by monthly payment savings, but it remains a simplified nominal-dollar estimate.

Why does the calculator ask how long I will keep the loan?

Refinancing costs and payment savings occur at different times. The holding-period result compares payments made and the remaining balance at the end of your selected period. A refinance may look different over two years than over ten.

Should I pay closing costs upfront or finance them?

Paying them upfront uses cash at closing. Financing them increases the new loan amount and interest paid. Compare the total costs and the amount of cash you need, not only the monthly payment.

Can a longer term reduce my monthly payment?

Yes. A longer repayment period can lower the scheduled payment while extending the time you carry mortgage debt and potentially increasing total interest.

Does a no-closing-cost refinance mean there are no costs?

Not necessarily. Costs may be offset by lender credits in exchange for a higher interest rate, or handled through another loan structure. Review the Loan Estimate to see the rate, lender credits and cash to close.

Does this calculator include taxes and insurance?

No. It compares principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and escrow deposits are excluded.

Does this calculator estimate a cash-out refinance?

No. It assumes the new loan begins with the current mortgage payoff, with optional closing costs added. Use the Cash-Out Refinance Calculator for a cash-out scenario.

Is the result a lender quote?

No. It is an educational estimate. A lender will determine the actual payoff, property value, rate, costs, loan terms and qualification.

How mortgage refinancing works

Refinancing replaces your existing mortgage with a new mortgage. Borrowers may refinance to seek a lower interest rate, change the loan term, change the monthly payment, or use other loan features.

This calculator compares principal and interest on the existing balance with a new fixed-rate loan. It also subtracts estimated closing costs when showing the simple net benefit.

Understanding break-even

The simple break-even period is estimated by dividing refinance closing costs by the monthly payment reduction. For example, $6,000 of costs divided by $200 of monthly savings gives a 30-month break-even period.

Why a lower payment may not mean lower total interest

If you refinance into a longer term, the monthly payment can fall while the new loan remains outstanding for more years. Compare both monthly payment and total interest before making a decision.

Mortgage refinance FAQs

What is mortgage refinancing?

It replaces an existing mortgage with a new loan, often to change the rate, payment, term, or loan features.

How is break-even calculated?

This calculator divides estimated closing costs by monthly principal-and-interest savings. It is a simplified estimate.

Can refinancing lower my payment?

Yes, depending on the new rate and term. A longer term can lower payments but may increase total interest.

Does refinancing always save money?

No. Results depend on rates, balance, remaining term, closing costs, and how long you keep the new loan.

Should closing costs be included?

Including them provides a more realistic break-even estimate, although actual costs vary by lender and transaction.

Are taxes and insurance included?

No. The comparison focuses on principal and interest. Escrow costs can behave differently after refinancing.

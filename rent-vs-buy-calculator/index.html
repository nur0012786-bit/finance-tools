CALCULATION METHOD

How this US rent vs buy calculator works

The calculator projects your total rent payments over the selected number of years, applying the annual rent-increase assumption at the start of each year. For buying, it models the down payment, closing costs, mortgage principal and interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance and maintenance.

It also estimates the remaining mortgage balance after the selected period. Home value is projected using your appreciation assumption, then estimated selling costs are deducted to calculate sale proceeds. The resulting home equity is shown separately so you can see how principal repayment and home-price changes affect the ownership scenario.

Finally, the calculator estimates the opportunity cost of the initial cash used for the down payment and closing costs. This represents the hypothetical investment growth that cash could have generated at the annual return assumption if it had remained invested instead.

Why the time horizon matters

Buying and selling a home involve transaction costs. The CFPB notes that buying and selling can be expensive and that moving again within a few years can make homeownership more costly. What costs should buyers include?

A mortgage principal-and-interest payment is only part of the ownership budget. Worked scenario

For an illustrative scenario, enter $2,500 monthly rent, a $400,000 home, an $80,000 down payment, a 6.5% mortgage rate and a 7-year horizon. Then test different rent-growth and home-appreciation assumptions. The purpose is to see how sensitive the comparison is to the assumptions, not to identify a universal answer.

Renting and buying have different risks

Renting generally avoids most property maintenance and repair responsibilities, while buying transfers those responsibilities to the homeowner. Homeowners also take on the risk that property values can decline. Renters may have more flexibility if they expect to move, while buyers face transaction costs when purchasing and later selling. citeturn0search1turn0search2

FAQ

US rent vs buy calculator FAQs

Does buying always cost less than renting?

No. The result depends on rent growth, home appreciation, mortgage rate, taxes, insurance, maintenance, transaction costs, investment assumptions and the time you remain in the property.

Why does the calculator include maintenance?

Homeowners are responsible for repairs and maintenance. The CFPB recommends budgeting for these costs because repairs can range from routine expenses to major replacements. citeturn0search0

Why are selling costs included?

Selling a home can involve commissions, taxes and other transaction costs. Including an estimated selling-cost percentage helps represent the cost of exiting the ownership scenario.

Does mortgage principal count as a cost?

Principal is a cash outflow, but it also reduces the mortgage balance and therefore contributes to home equity. The calculator shows both cash outflows and ending equity instead of treating principal as pure expense.

Does the calculator include tax deductions?

No. Tax effects depend on individual circumstances and applicable federal and state rules. The model leaves them out rather than assuming a tax benefit applies to every buyer.

Does it include property taxes and insurance?

Yes. You can enter an annual property-tax rate and annual homeowners-insurance cost. Actual taxes and insurance can change over time.

Does it include HOA fees?

No. Add HOA costs to your ownership budget separately when evaluating a property where they apply.

Why is opportunity cost included?

The down payment and closing costs use cash that could otherwise remain invested. The calculator estimates the hypothetical growth of that initial cash at the return assumption you enter.

Should I use one set of assumptions?

No. Future rent, home prices, investment returns, maintenance and other costs are uncertain. Testing several reasonable scenarios can show how sensitive the comparison is to those assumptions. citeturn0search1turn0search2

Is this a financial recommendation?

No. It is a scenario calculator. Consider your cash reserves, mobility, household budget, maintenance responsibilities and long-term plans when evaluating housing choices.

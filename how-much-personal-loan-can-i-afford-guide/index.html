Educational finance guidance

Affordability is about whether the required payment fits within your overall budget and obligations. A lender's approval amount is not the same thing as a personal affordability target.

Start with your monthly cash flow

Review take-home income, essential expenses, existing debt payments, savings needs and irregular costs before considering a new loan payment.

Consider the full loan cost

Look beyond the advertised monthly payment. Check the interest rate, APR, fees, repayment term and total amount scheduled to be repaid.

Why a longer term changes the picture

A longer term can reduce the scheduled monthly payment while increasing the time the balance remains outstanding and potentially increasing total interest.

Lender qualification is different

Lenders may use their own underwriting criteria, including income, credit history, existing obligations and loan-specific rules. Approval does not establish that the payment fits comfortably within your personal budget.

Illustrative example

A borrower comparing two loans may find that the longer term creates a smaller required payment but a higher overall repayment amount. The useful comparison is whether the payment works in the budget and what it costs over the full term.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

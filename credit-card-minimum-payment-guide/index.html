Credit Card Minimum Payment Explained

Learn what a credit card minimum payment is, why paying only the minimum can take longer, and what to review on your statement.

Educational finance guidance

The minimum payment is the amount the card issuer requires you to pay by the due date to keep the account current, subject to the card's agreement.

How the minimum payment is determined

Card issuers can use formulas described in the card agreement, which may incorporate a percentage of the balance, interest, fees or a minimum-dollar amount.

Why minimum payments can extend repayment

Paying only the minimum can reduce the balance slowly, especially when the APR is high or new purchases continue.

Interest and the statement cycle

Interest calculations depend on the card agreement and applicable rules. Review the statement for the balance, APR, fees, minimum payment and due date.

Paying more than the minimum

Additional payments can generally reduce the balance faster, subject to the account terms and how payments are applied.

Illustrative example

A card with a persistent balance can take substantially longer to repay when payments are near the minimum than when the cardholder makes larger fixed payments.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

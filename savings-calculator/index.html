How a savings calculator works

A savings calculator combines your starting balance, regular deposits, interest rate and time period to estimate how much money you could have in the future. Regular deposits can make a substantial difference because each deposit can have additional time to earn interest.

Why start saving early?

Time gives your savings more opportunities to earn interest. Even when the monthly deposit is relatively small, consistent saving over several years can build a larger balance than occasional deposits.

Savings example

If you start with $5,000, add $250 each month and earn an assumed 4% annual rate for five years, the calculator estimates the balance using those assumptions. Your actual result will depend on the account's rate, compounding method and deposit timing.

Frequently asked questions

What does a savings calculator calculate?

It estimates a future savings balance using your starting balance, regular deposits, interest rate, compounding frequency and time period.

Does this calculator include monthly deposits?

Yes. Enter the amount you plan to deposit each month to include regular contributions in the estimate.

Is the calculated interest guaranteed?

No. The result is an estimate based on the rate you enter. Savings account rates can change and account terms vary.

Does the calculator include bank fees?

No. Monthly fees, account fees, taxes and other charges are not included.

What happens if I increase my monthly savings?

A higher monthly deposit generally increases the projected future balance because more money is added to the account.

Can I use this for an emergency fund?

Yes, it can help illustrate how regular deposits could build an emergency fund, but your target should reflect your own expenses and circumstances.

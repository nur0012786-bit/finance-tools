How this US mortgage affordability calculator works

This calculator estimates a home price from gross monthly household income, existing monthly debt, cash down payment, mortgage rate and term, property-tax rate, insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues, and two DTI assumptions.

Housing and total debt-to-income (DTI)

Housing DTI compares the proposed monthly housing expense with gross monthly income. Total DTI adds other recurring monthly debt payments to that housing expense and divides the result by gross monthly income. This calculator checks both limits and uses the lower monthly housing budget.

  • Housing budget: gross monthly income × housing DTI limit.
  • Total-DTI housing budget: gross monthly income × total DTI limit − other monthly debt payments.
  • Available monthly housing budget: the lower of the two values above.

Monthly housing costs included

For each possible home price, the calculator estimates principal and interest on the purchase price less the fixed cash down payment, then adds property taxes, homeowners insurance, the monthly mortgage-insurance amount you enter, and monthly HOA or condo dues. Mortgage insurance is an explicit user-entered estimate because actual premiums depend on the loan program and borrower details; the calculator does not invent a PMI quote.

The property tax estimate applies the entered annual percentage to the full home price and divides by 12. Actual taxable value and assessments may differ by location. Homeowners insurance is entered annually and converted to a monthly amount.

Worked example using the default inputs

With $90,000 of annual gross income, $500 of other monthly debt, $30,000 down, a 6.5% fixed rate, a 30-year term, 1.2% annual property tax and $1,800 annual homeowners insurance, the estimated maximum home price is about $292,271. The result is based on the 28% housing DTI assumption, which allows $2,100 in monthly housing expense. At the estimated price, the mortgage is about $262,271; monthly principal and interest is about $1,658, property tax about $292, and homeowners insurance $150. Mortgage insurance and HOA defaults are $0.

DTI ratios are planning assumptions, not universal approval limits

The 28% housing and 36% total DTI defaults are budgeting assumptions you can change. Mortgage underwriting limits depend on the loan program and the borrower's full financial profile. For example, Fannie Mae's current guidance describes different maximum total DTI limits for manually underwritten loans and Desktop Underwriter casefiles; those are underwriting rules, not a personal spending recommendation.

What this estimate does not decide

This is not a mortgage preapproval or personal budget recommendation. It does not model closing costs, cash reserves, utilities, maintenance, flood or supplemental insurance, future tax increases, adjustable-rate changes, loan-program fees, property eligibility or lender-specific underwriting. The CFPB recommends comparing the total expected home payment and your wider household budget, not borrowing the maximum amount a lender may approve.

Mortgage affordability FAQs

How much house can I afford?

This calculator estimates a maximum home price from the income, debts, down payment, rate, term, property tax, insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and DTI assumptions you enter. A lender's qualifying amount and your comfortable personal budget can be different.

What is housing DTI?

Housing DTI is the proposed monthly housing expense divided by gross monthly income. This calculator includes principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, entered mortgage insurance and HOA dues.

What is total DTI?

Total DTI includes the proposed housing expense plus the other recurring monthly debt payments entered on the form, divided by gross monthly income.

Are 28% and 36% DTI rules?

No. They are editable planning assumptions, not universal approval rules. Lender limits vary by loan type, underwriting method and borrower circumstances.

Does the calculator estimate PMI?

No. Enter a monthly mortgage-insurance quote or planning estimate from your lender. Actual PMI or other mortgage-insurance charges depend on loan type, down payment, credit and other factors.

Why does a larger down payment increase the estimated home price?

With the same monthly housing budget, a larger down payment reduces the mortgage principal needed for a given home price. That can support a higher purchase price under the same assumptions, but it also uses more cash upfront.

Does this include property tax and homeowners insurance?

Yes. Property tax is estimated from the annual percentage of home price you enter. Homeowners insurance is based on the annual premium you enter. Actual assessments and premiums can differ.

Does this include HOA fees and mortgage insurance?

Yes, when you enter those monthly amounts. They are not automatically inferred because actual costs vary widely by property, loan and borrower.

Does this include maintenance, utilities and closing costs?

No. Those expenses are excluded from the DTI model. Consider them separately when deciding what monthly housing payment is comfortable for your household.

Does this calculator provide mortgage preapproval?

No. It does not evaluate credit, employment, assets, reserves, property eligibility or lender-specific underwriting. Only a lender can assess an application and issue a preapproval.

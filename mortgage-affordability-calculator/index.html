How this US mortgage affordability calculator works

This calculator converts annual gross income to a monthly figure, then applies two planning limits: a housing DTI limit and a total DTI limit. The total-DTI calculation subtracts the monthly debt payments you enter before determining the amount available for housing.

For each possible home price, the calculator estimates principal and interest on the mortgage amount after your down payment, adds estimated property tax and homeowners insurance, and checks that the result stays within the lower of those two monthly housing limits. It then searches for the highest price that fits those assumptions.

What the result means

The estimated maximum home price is a mathematical planning result, not an approval amount. It reflects only the inputs on this page. A lender may use different DTI limits, qualifying income, tax and insurance figures, mortgage insurance, loan-program rules, or underwriting requirements.

Worked example

With $90,000 of annual gross income, $500 of existing monthly debt, a $30,000 down payment, a 6.5% rate, a 30-year term, 1.2% annual property tax, and $1,800 annual homeowners insurance, the default assumptions produce an estimated home price of about $292,271. That corresponds to an estimated $262,271 mortgage, about $1,658 of monthly principal and interest, about $292 of monthly property tax, and $150 of monthly insurance. The example uses the calculator's default 28% housing DTI and 36% total DTI assumptions.

Costs not modeled

This estimate does not add PMI, FHA/VA/USDA mortgage-insurance or guarantee fees, HOA dues, maintenance, utilities, closing costs, lender fees, or other property-specific expenses. If any of these materially affect your budget, use a separate calculator or adjust your planning assumptions.

Mortgage affordability FAQs

How much house can I afford?

This calculator estimates a maximum home price from the income, debts, down payment, rate, taxes, insurance, and DTI limits you enter. A lender's qualifying amount can be different.

What is housing DTI?

Housing DTI is monthly housing expense divided by gross monthly income. Here it is used as a planning cap for principal, interest, property tax, and homeowners insurance.

What is total DTI?

Total DTI includes the estimated housing payment plus the recurring monthly debt payments entered on the form, divided by gross monthly income.

Why can a larger down payment change the result?

A larger down payment reduces the mortgage needed for a given home price. That can reduce principal-and-interest expense and therefore allow a higher home price under the same monthly housing limit.

Does this calculator include PMI?

No. PMI is not modeled in this calculation. Conventional borrowers who put less than 20% down may have mortgage insurance, depending on the loan and lender.

Does this calculator provide mortgage preapproval?

No. It is an educational planning estimate and does not evaluate credit, employment, assets, reserves, property eligibility, or lender-specific underwriting.

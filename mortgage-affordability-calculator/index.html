How this mortgage affordability calculator works

The calculator first estimates your gross monthly income and two debt limits. The housing limit uses the housing DTI assumption, while the total debt limit accounts for your existing monthly debt payments.

It then estimates the highest home price that can fit within those limits while accounting for principal and interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, and your down payment.

Example

If you earn $90,000 per year, have $500 in monthly debt payments, put $30,000 down, and use a 6.5% mortgage rate, the result provides a planning estimate based on the DTI and cost assumptions you enter.

Why the estimate can differ from a lender

Lenders may consider credit history, employment and income documentation, assets and reserves, loan program rules, mortgage insurance, property characteristics, and other recurring obligations. Rates and qualifying standards can also vary.

Mortgage affordability FAQs

How much house can I afford?

Your affordable home price depends on income, debt, down payment, rate, taxes, insurance, and lender qualification rules.

What is housing DTI?

Housing DTI compares monthly housing costs with gross monthly income. 28% is a common planning assumption, but actual limits vary.

What is total DTI?

Total DTI includes housing costs plus other recurring debt payments divided by gross monthly income.

Does a larger down payment help?

It reduces the mortgage needed for a given home price and can reduce borrowing costs, although other qualification rules still apply.

Does this calculator provide preapproval?

No. It is a planning estimate and does not replace a lender's underwriting or preapproval process.

Are taxes and insurance included?

Yes. The estimate uses the tax rate and annual insurance amount you enter. Actual costs vary by location and property.

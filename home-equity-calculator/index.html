How home equity is calculated

Home equity is the portion of your home's value that is not covered by mortgage and other property-secured debt. The basic calculation is home value minus secured debt.

For example, if a home is worth $400,000 and the mortgage balance is $250,000, estimated equity is $150,000. That equals 37.5% of the home's estimated value.

Understanding LTV

Loan-to-value ratio compares secured debt with the property's estimated value. If a home is worth $400,000 and total secured debt is $250,000, the LTV is 62.5%.

Potential borrowing room

A lender may use a maximum combined LTV when evaluating a home equity loan or HELOC. This calculator applies the target percentage you enter, but actual limits vary by lender and borrower.

What can change your equity?

Mortgage principal payments can increase equity, while a lower home value can reduce it. Additional borrowing against the property also reduces available equity.

Home equity FAQs

What is home equity?

It is the estimated home value minus mortgages and other liens secured by the property.

How is equity calculated?

Subtract total property-secured debt from the estimated market value of the home.

What is LTV?

LTV is secured debt divided by home value, expressed as a percentage.

Can I borrow all my equity?

Usually a lender will apply a maximum LTV or combined LTV and other qualification requirements.

Does home value affect equity?

Yes. Changes in estimated property value can change your equity even if the mortgage balance does not change.

Is this an approval calculator?

No. It provides an educational estimate and does not determine lender approval or an actual credit limit.

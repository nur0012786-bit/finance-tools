CALCULATION METHOD

How this home equity calculator works

Home equity is the estimated value of a property minus the outstanding balances of loans secured by that property. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes equity as the value of the home minus the amount owed on the mortgage. If there is a mortgage, a HELOC or another lien, include the applicable outstanding balances when estimating the equity remaining after debt.

1. Estimate current home equity

Estimated net home equity = current home value − current first-mortgage balance − other secured balances − drawn HELOC balance

The current first-mortgage balance and original first-mortgage loan amount are not interchangeable. Use the current balance to estimate the debt still owed today. Use the original first-mortgage amount for the Fannie Mae-style CLTV and HCLTV illustrations described below.

2. Understand the balance-based debt-to-value ratio

This page calculates outstanding-debt-to-value by dividing the current outstanding balances of the mortgage, other secured loans and drawn HELOC by the home value you enter. It is a simple current-balance snapshot for planning, not a formal lender LTV or CLTV determination.

3. Understand CLTV

Fannie Mae's published CLTV methodology for mortgage underwriting uses the original first-mortgage loan amount, the drawn portion (outstanding principal balance) of any HELOC, and the unpaid principal balance of other closed-end subordinate financing. The total is divided by the applicable property value. This calculator uses the entered property value as a planning denominator; an actual lender calculation may use the lower of the sales price or appraised value and may apply transaction-specific rules.

Illustrative CLTV numerator = original first-mortgage amount + drawn HELOC balance + other closed-end subordinate balances

4. Understand HCLTV

When a mortgage package includes a HELOC, Fannie Mae's HCLTV methodology uses the original first-mortgage loan amount, the full HELOC credit limit whether or not the line is fully drawn, and the unpaid principal balance of other closed-end subordinate financing. That is why the calculator asks for both the existing HELOC credit limit and amount drawn. The credit limit is used for HCLTV; the drawn amount is used in the current outstanding-debt and CLTV calculations.

Illustrative HCLTV numerator = original first-mortgage amount + full existing HELOC credit limit + other closed-end subordinate balances

These are Fannie Mae guide concepts for its mortgage eligibility framework. They are not a promise that every home-equity lender will use the same calculation or target limit.

5. Estimate additional HELOC limit room

The calculator multiplies the home value by your selected target HCLTV percentage, then subtracts the existing HCLTV exposure amount. It displays zero if the existing exposure is already at or above the target. This is only mathematical headroom under the chosen assumption, not a maximum credit line offered by a bank.

Lenders also assess the property valuation, credit history, income, debts, repayment ability, lien position, property type and loan terms. Appraisal, application, title, closing and other fees may apply. A lender's written disclosures and underwriting decision control the actual offer.

HOME-EQUITY OPTIONS

HELOC, home equity loan or cash-out refinance?

A HELOC is generally a revolving line secured by the home. You may draw and repay funds during the draw period within the line's rules. A home equity loan generally provides a lump sum and is repaid under a set schedule. A cash-out refinance replaces the existing mortgage with a larger mortgage and provides the difference in cash. These products can have different interest rates, closing costs, payment structures and repayment risks.

The CFPB explains that a HELOC's credit limit is generally based on a percentage of home value minus the amount owed on the mortgage. It also warns that the home is collateral, so missed payments can put the property at risk. Read the CFPB's September 2026 HELOC booklet.

WHAT CAN CHANGE HOME EQUITY?

Factors that can increase or reduce estimated equity

  • Principal payments: Reducing loan principal generally increases the equity remaining after debt.
  • Home value changes: Appreciation can increase estimated equity; depreciation can reduce it.
  • New liens or secured borrowing: A new loan or HELOC draw increases the debt secured by the property.
  • Existing HELOC credit limits: An unused line may still count in HCLTV underwriting calculations, even though it is not current drawn debt.
  • Property valuation: An online estimate may differ from a lender-accepted appraisal or valuation.
  • Closing and lender costs: Fees and financed costs can affect the actual amount borrowed and cash received.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Home equity calculator FAQs

How do I calculate home equity?

Subtract the current outstanding balances of mortgages and other property-secured debt from the home's estimated current value. This calculator includes the drawn balance of any existing HELOC in that debt total.

What is the difference between home equity and borrowing capacity?

Home equity is the estimated value left after current secured debt. Borrowing capacity is a lender's decision based on property value, loan-to-value limits, credit, income, debts, repayment ability and other underwriting factors. They are not the same.

What is the difference between LTV and CLTV?

LTV generally compares a first mortgage with property value. CLTV accounts for subordinate financing as well. Fannie Mae's CLTV formula uses the original first-mortgage amount, drawn HELOC balances and other closed-end subordinate balances.

What is HCLTV?

HCLTV is a home equity combined loan-to-value measure used when a mortgage financing package includes a HELOC. Fannie Mae's formula counts the full HELOC credit limit, whether or not the full line is drawn, plus the original first mortgage amount and other subordinate balances.

Why do you ask for the original first-mortgage amount?

It is separate from the current unpaid balance. Fannie Mae's published CLTV and HCLTV calculations use the original first-mortgage loan amount. Use the original note or closing documents if you need that figure for an underwriting-style estimate.

Why does an unused HELOC limit affect HCLTV?

Fannie Mae's HCLTV methodology counts the full HELOC credit line, even when some or all of it has not been drawn. The outstanding drawn portion is used in the CLTV calculation and current debt balance.

What target HCLTV should I use?

There is no universal target for every lender or product. The 80% default is an illustrative input, not an industry-wide approval standard. Use a target supplied by the lender you are evaluating, or test several assumptions.

Does the calculator determine whether I qualify for a HELOC?

No. It estimates equity, ratios and mathematical headroom. The lender may require an appraisal and will assess credit, income, debt obligations, repayment ability and product-specific rules.

Can my home's value change the result?

Yes. A higher or lower valuation changes the estimated equity and ratios. Lenders may rely on an appraisal or another accepted valuation method rather than the estimate entered here.

Can I borrow all of my home equity?

Not necessarily. Lenders may cap the amount based on CLTV or HCLTV and apply other limits and qualification rules. The home's collateral value does not establish that the borrower can repay the loan.

Educational finance guidance

Credit card debt is revolving debt, so the balance can change with purchases, payments, fees and interest. A payoff strategy should account for the account's actual terms.

Know the balance and APR

Start with the current balance, interest rate or APR, minimum payment and any promotional terms.

Choose a repayment method

Common strategies include prioritizing the highest-rate balance or focusing on the smallest balance while maintaining minimum payments on other accounts.

Avoid adding new balances

A repayment plan can be undermined when new purchases continue to increase the balance. Track spending and payment activity together.

Understand promotional rates

Balance transfers or promotional APR periods can change the interest calculation and may include fees or expiration dates.

Illustrative example

A cardholder with a $5,000 balance can compare different monthly payment levels to see how the estimated payoff period and total interest change.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

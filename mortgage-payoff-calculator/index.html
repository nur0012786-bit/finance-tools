HOW IT WORKS

How this mortgage payoff calculator works

The calculator first computes the regular principal-and-interest payment from your current balance, annual interest rate and remaining term. It then simulates the mortgage month by month, calculating interest from the outstanding balance and applying the remaining payment to principal.

For the accelerated scenario, the calculator subtracts the one-time extra payment from the starting balance and adds the extra monthly amount to each scheduled principal-and-interest payment. It continues the simulation until the balance reaches zero.

Important: this is an amortization estimate, not a lender payoff quote. It assumes a fixed interest rate and does not model escrow, changing rates, late fees, servicing rules, or unusual loan features.

WHY EXTRA PRINCIPAL CHANGES THE SCHEDULE

Why extra mortgage payments can reduce interest

Mortgage interest is calculated using the outstanding principal balance. When an additional payment is applied to principal, the balance falls sooner, so future interest is calculated on a smaller amount. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that principal payments reduce the balance and that amortization shifts more of a scheduled payment toward principal as the balance declines.

Freddie Mac also describes extra principal payments as one way borrowers can shorten the mortgage term and reduce interest in a typical fixed-rate mortgage.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Example mortgage payoff scenario

Suppose a borrower has a $300,000 balance at 6.25% with 25 years remaining and adds $300 to the regular monthly principal-and-interest payment. The calculator compares the original amortization schedule with the accelerated schedule and reports the modeled months saved and interest saved.

A separate one-time payment can be tested as well. For example, entering $10,000 as the one-time extra payment models an immediate reduction in the starting principal before the monthly simulation begins.

The result is sensitive to the interest rate, remaining term, payment amount and timing of extra payments. Your servicer's actual payment application and loan terms control the real outcome.

PAYOFF AMOUNT VS. BALANCE

Your mortgage balance is not always the payoff amount

If you want to completely satisfy your mortgage on a specific date, request an official payoff statement from your mortgage servicer. The CFPB explains that a payoff amount can differ from the current principal balance because it can include interest due through the payoff date and other applicable charges.

This calculator estimates amortization and early-payoff timing; it does not calculate the exact amount needed to release the mortgage lien on a particular date.

PREPAYMENT CONSIDERATIONS

Check your mortgage terms before paying extra

Many borrowers can make additional principal payments, but the exact rules depend on the mortgage. Check your loan documents or ask your servicer how extra payments are credited. CFPB guidance notes that borrowers should verify whether a prepayment penalty applies and whether extra payments are being applied to principal.

If your mortgage has a prepayment penalty, an early payoff or certain large extra payments could create a fee. The calculator does not estimate prepayment penalties.

FAQ

US mortgage payoff calculator FAQs

Does extra principal reduce mortgage interest?

On a typical fixed-rate mortgage, reducing the principal balance sooner generally reduces the amount on which future interest is calculated.

How much extra should I pay each month?

There is no universal amount. Use the calculator to test different extra-payment amounts and compare the modeled payoff time and interest. Your budget, emergency savings, other debts and loan terms are separate considerations.

What happens if I make a one-time extra payment?

A lump-sum principal payment reduces the modeled starting balance and can shorten the remaining payoff period and reduce modeled interest.

Does this calculator include property taxes and insurance?

No. It focuses on mortgage principal and interest. Escrow for property taxes and homeowners insurance is excluded from the payoff simulation.

Is this an exact mortgage payoff quote?

No. An official payoff amount can include interest through a specified date and other applicable charges. Request a payoff statement from your mortgage servicer when you need an exact figure.

Can I use this for an adjustable-rate mortgage?

This calculator is designed for a simplified fixed-rate amortization model. An adjustable-rate mortgage can change the payment and interest rate over time, so this model may not represent its future payoff schedule.

Could my mortgage have a prepayment penalty?

Some mortgages can have prepayment penalties, depending on the loan terms and applicable rules. Review your mortgage documents or ask your servicer. This calculator does not include a penalty.

How are extra payments applied?

This calculator assumes extra amounts are applied directly to principal. Confirm with your servicer how additional payments are credited under your loan terms.

Educational estimate: This calculator is for planning only. It does not provide a lender payoff quote or financial advice. Verify your loan terms, prepayment provisions and payment application rules with your mortgage servicer.

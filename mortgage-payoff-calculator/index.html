HOW IT WORKS

How the mortgage payoff calculator works

The calculator first estimates the normal fixed-rate mortgage payment from your current balance, interest rate and remaining term. It then simulates monthly interest and principal payments while applying your extra monthly amount and optional one-time payment.

The accelerated scenario shows the estimated payoff time and interest under those assumptions. Actual results can differ because mortgage servicing, payment timing, escrow, fees and lender-specific rules vary.

EXAMPLE

Example mortgage payoff strategy

Suppose your remaining balance is $300,000 at 6.25% with 25 years left. Adding $300 per month toward principal can shorten the modeled payoff period and reduce modeled interest compared with making only the scheduled payment.

The calculator lets you test different extra-payment amounts so you can compare scenarios before discussing a strategy with your lender.

FAQ

Mortgage payoff calculator FAQs

Does extra principal reduce interest?

On a typical fixed-rate mortgage, reducing principal sooner generally reduces the balance on which future interest is calculated.

Can I make extra monthly payments?

Many mortgages permit additional principal payments, but check your mortgage documents and lender for applicable rules.

What about a one-time payment?

A lump-sum principal payment can reduce the balance immediately and may shorten the modeled payoff period.

Does this include escrow?

No. Taxes, insurance and HOA costs are excluded because they do not directly determine mortgage principal-and-interest payoff.

Is this a lender payoff quote?

No. A lender's payoff quote may include accrued interest, fees and other charges that this calculator does not model.

Can I use this for any mortgage?

It is designed for a simplified fixed-rate amortization model. Adjustable-rate mortgages and unusual loan structures may require a different calculation.

Educational estimate: This calculator is for planning only. It does not provide a lender payoff quote or financial advice. Check your loan documents and lender terms before making additional principal payments.

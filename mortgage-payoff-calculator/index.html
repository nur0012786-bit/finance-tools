CALCULATION METHOD

How this mortgage payoff calculator works

This calculator compares a standard fixed-rate mortgage schedule with an accelerated scenario. It uses the current principal balance, annual interest rate and remaining term to calculate scheduled monthly principal and interest. The accelerated scenario keeps that scheduled payment and adds the extra principal amounts entered.

Regular monthly payment formula

Monthly P&I = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

  • P is the current principal balance.
  • r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100).
  • n is the remaining number of monthly payments (years × 12).

The calculation assumes a constant interest rate and regular monthly payments. It does not recalculate the scheduled payment after each extra payment; it assumes the borrower continues paying the original principal-and-interest amount plus any extra principal.

How extra monthly payments are modeled

The extra monthly amount is added to the scheduled principal-and-interest payment and applied to principal every month. As the balance declines, less interest accrues in later periods, so more of the total payment reduces principal.

How the one-time payment is modeled

The one-time payment is deducted from the starting balance immediately before the first modeled monthly interest calculation. A real payment may post on a different date, so payment timing and servicer posting rules can change the actual result. The one-time payment must be smaller than the starting principal balance. This is not a full payoff quote.

The calculator does not automatically recast the mortgage after a lump-sum payment. It keeps the original scheduled principal-and-interest payment, modeling a shorter payoff period rather than a lower required payment.

WHY PRINCIPAL PAYMENTS MATTER

Why extra mortgage principal can reduce interest

Mortgage interest is generally calculated using the unpaid principal balance. Extra money correctly applied to principal reduces the balance earlier, which can reduce interest in later periods. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau advises borrowers to check that extra payments are applied to principal and explains that additional principal can help repay the mortgage sooner and with less interest. Read CFPB mortgage-servicing guidance.

Freddie Mac describes paying extra toward principal, rounding up payments and biweekly payment arrangements as possible ways to shorten a mortgage, while advising borrowers to discuss arrangements with their lender. Read Freddie Mac's guide to paying off a mortgage faster.

Confirm how your servicer posts extra payments

Ask how to designate principal-only payments, when the extra amount is credited and whether it changes your next due date. Follow the servicer's written instructions and check your next statement to confirm the payment was applied as intended.

PAYOFF AMOUNT VS. CURRENT BALANCE

Your mortgage balance is not the same as a payoff quote

The current principal balance is not necessarily the amount required to satisfy a mortgage on a particular date. A payoff amount can include interest due through the intended payoff date and other unpaid charges, and may include a prepayment penalty if the loan terms provide for one. The CFPB explains this distinction and says borrowers can request an official payoff statement from their servicer. Read CFPB guidance on payoff amounts.

Use this calculator to compare amortization scenarios. When selling, refinancing or paying off the entire loan, obtain a dated payoff statement from your mortgage servicer rather than using this estimate as the amount to send.

PREPAYMENT PENALTIES AND TRADE-OFFS

Check your mortgage terms before making large extra payments

Not all mortgages have a prepayment penalty. The CFPB explains that a penalty may apply in certain circumstances, often when the full mortgage is paid off within a specified early period or when a large lump-sum payment is made. Small additional principal payments do not normally trigger a penalty, but confirm the terms in your own mortgage documents. Read the CFPB prepayment-penalty guide.

Paying down a mortgage faster is not automatically the best use of every spare dollar. Consider emergency savings, higher-interest debt, cash-flow needs, possible investment alternatives and tax or loan-specific considerations. This calculator compares mortgage amortization only; it does not recommend how to allocate your money.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Mortgage payoff calculator FAQs

How much time can I save by paying extra on my mortgage?

It depends on your balance, interest rate, remaining term, extra amount and payment timing. Enter different monthly or lump-sum principal payments to compare modeled payoff times.

Does paying extra principal reduce mortgage interest?

On a typical fixed-rate amortizing mortgage, applying extra money to principal reduces the balance used to calculate future interest. Your servicer must apply the payment according to your mortgage terms and payment instructions.

Should I make a monthly extra payment or a lump-sum payment?

This calculator can compare either method or a combination. Its lump-sum scenario assumes the payment reduces principal immediately before the first modeled monthly interest period.

Does a lump-sum payment lower my required monthly payment?

Not automatically. This calculator keeps the original scheduled principal-and-interest payment and models a shorter payoff period. A mortgage recast, if available and approved by the servicer, is a separate process that may recalculate the required payment.

Is my current mortgage balance the payoff amount?

No. A dated payoff amount can include accrued interest through the payoff date and other unpaid charges. Request an official payoff statement from your servicer when you need to satisfy the loan in full.

Can my lender charge a prepayment penalty?

Some mortgages have prepayment penalties under specific terms and conditions. Review your note and disclosures or ask your servicer. The calculator does not estimate a penalty.

Does this calculator include taxes and homeowners insurance?

No. It compares principal-and-interest amortization only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and escrow are excluded.

Can I use this for an adjustable-rate mortgage?

This calculator assumes a fixed interest rate for the modeled period. An adjustable-rate mortgage can reset, so its future payments and payoff schedule may differ.

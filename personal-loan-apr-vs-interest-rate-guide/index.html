APR VS. RATE

What is a personal loan interest rate?

The interest rate is the rate used to calculate interest charged on the loan under its terms. For an installment loan, the rate helps determine the scheduled payment together with the loan balance, repayment term and payment structure.

What is APR?

Annual percentage rate, or APR, is a standardized way to express the cost of borrowing that can incorporate the interest rate and certain finance charges. Because applicable APR rules depend on the type of credit and the transaction, the lender's disclosures are the authoritative source for the actual APR.

Why can APR be higher than the interest rate?

When a loan includes certain finance charges, the APR may be higher than the stated interest rate because APR is intended to reflect more of the borrowing cost than the rate alone. A loan with no applicable finance charges beyond interest may have an APR close to its interest rate, subject to the specific calculation.

Which number matters when comparing personal loans?

Both matter, but neither should be considered in isolation. The interest rate helps you understand the rate used to calculate interest, while APR can help compare the cost of credit when the loans have different fees. Also compare the amount borrowed, repayment term, scheduled payment, upfront charges and total amount repaid.

Illustrative example

Imagine two hypothetical personal loan offers with the same $10,000 amount and three-year term. Offer A has a lower interest rate but a larger upfront finance charge. Offer B has a slightly higher interest rate but fewer applicable fees. The APR can help make the cost comparison more meaningful, while the monthly payment still depends on the actual rate, amount, term and payment structure.

This example is educational only. Actual APR calculations and disclosed charges depend on the lender, loan and applicable requirements.

APR does not mean the monthly payment

APR is an annualized measure used for comparing borrowing costs; it is not the same thing as the scheduled monthly payment. Two loans can have similar APRs but different payment amounts when the loan amounts or repayment terms differ.

What to review in a personal loan offer

  • Interest rate and whether it is fixed or variable
  • APR shown in the lender's disclosures
  • Loan amount and repayment term
  • Scheduled monthly payment
  • Origination and other applicable finance charges
  • Total amount scheduled to be repaid
  • Late-payment terms and any applicable prepayment provisions

Frequently asked questions

Is APR always higher than the interest rate?

Not necessarily. APR can include certain finance charges, but the actual relationship depends on the loan structure and applicable calculation rules.

Can I use APR to predict my exact payment?

Not by itself. The actual payment depends on the loan amount, interest rate, term and payment structure. Use the lender's disclosed payment and loan terms for the actual offer.

Should I compare APRs from different loan offers?

APR can be useful for comparing borrowing costs when the loans are similar and the APRs are calculated under comparable rules. You should still review the full terms, fees, amount and repayment period.

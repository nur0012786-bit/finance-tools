APR VS. INTEREST RATE

What is the interest rate on a personal loan?

The interest rate is the rate the lender charges for the money you borrow. On a typical fixed-rate installment loan, interest is calculated against the outstanding principal according to the agreement's payment schedule. The rate, loan amount, term and repayment structure work together to determine the scheduled payment and the interest charged over time.

The stated interest rate does not necessarily show every cost associated with obtaining the loan. A lender may also charge fees at or before origination. That is why the interest rate should be reviewed alongside the annual percentage rate (APR), the amount you actually receive and the full repayment terms.

What is APR?

APR means annual percentage rate. It is a yearly measure of the cost of credit that relates the amount and timing of the value you receive to the amount and timing of the payments you make. Under the federal Truth in Lending Act and Regulation Z, APR is calculated using prescribed methods and includes applicable finance charges in the calculation.

For a personal loan, the APR commonly reflects the interest rate plus finance charges that qualify for inclusion, such as certain origination or loan fees. However, APR does not necessarily include every fee or expense you could incur. Some charges are excluded under Regulation Z, and fees that arise only if you pay late or default are generally not part of the disclosed APR calculation. The specific treatment depends on the fee and the credit transaction.

The CFPB summarizes the difference as the interest rate being the cost of borrowing, while APR is a broader measure that can include certain additional loan fees. See its explanation of interest rate vs. APR and the current rules for finance charges under Regulation Z, § 1026.4.

Why can APR be higher than the interest rate?

APR can be higher when the loan has finance charges that are included in the APR calculation. An upfront origination fee is a common example. The borrower pays interest on the contractual loan amount, but may receive less money after the fee is deducted. APR reflects that difference between the amount of credit provided and the payments required.

If there are no finance charges that must be included beyond interest, the APR may be close to the stated interest rate. Do not assume that every fee is included or excluded automatically; the federal definition of a finance charge has specific rules and exceptions.

APR is not the monthly payment or the total dollar cost

APR is expressed as an annual rate. It is not the payment due every month, the total interest you will pay in dollars, or the total amount you will repay. The scheduled payment depends on the amount financed, interest rate, term, payment frequency and any required payment structure.

Two loans can have the same APR but different monthly payments because one has a larger balance or a different repayment term. A longer term often reduces the required monthly payment but can increase the total dollars of interest paid because the borrower makes payments for more months. A shorter term often has a higher monthly payment but can reduce total interest when other terms are comparable.

Which number should you compare when shopping?

Use APR as an important starting point when comparing similar offers, but do not select a loan based on APR alone. Compare the offers for the same net cash amount and a similar repayment term. Review the full disclosure and agreement for the following:

  • Interest rate: Is it fixed for the full term or can it change?
  • APR: What APR is disclosed, and what fees or finance charges are reflected in it?
  • Gross principal and net proceeds: How much will be deposited after any fees are deducted?
  • Origination and other fees: Which charges are required, optional, refundable or deducted upfront?
  • Monthly payment: What is the scheduled amount and when is the first payment due?
  • Total of payments: How much will you pay if you make every scheduled payment as agreed?
  • Term: How many payments are required, and does the longer term increase total cost?
  • Optional add-ons: Is credit insurance or another product optional, and what is its separate cost?
  • Late payment and prepayment terms: What charges or conditions may apply after the loan is made?

Ask each lender for the same amount and term where possible. Otherwise, an apparently lower payment may simply reflect a smaller loan or a longer repayment period. The CFPB recommends shopping around and reviewing the loan disclosures and documents before agreeing to a personal installment loan. See common personal installment loan fees.

Fixed APR vs. variable APR

A fixed interest rate generally stays the same for the agreed loan term, making scheduled principal-and-interest payments more predictable. A variable rate can change under the terms of the contract, which may change the amount of interest and potentially the payment. Ask what index or benchmark is used, the margin, when the rate can adjust and whether caps or floors apply.

Do not assume a rate described as “starting at” is the rate you will receive. The lender may assign a rate after reviewing your application, credit profile, income, debt obligations, loan amount and other underwriting factors. Review the rate and APR in the actual offer made to you.

How fees affect cash received and total repayment

A fee can affect your loan in different ways. If the lender deducts an origination fee from proceeds, you receive less money than the principal amount shown in the contract. If the fee is added to the loan balance, you may pay interest on the financed fee. If an optional product is added, it can raise the amount financed or require a separate payment.

Ask the lender to show the gross loan amount, each fee, net proceeds, amount financed for APR purposes, scheduled payment and total of payments. These figures help you understand both what you receive and what you must repay. See the Personal Loan Fees Guide for a closer look at origination and other charges.

Frequently asked questions

Is APR always higher than the interest rate?

No. APR can be higher when finance charges are included, but the relationship depends on the loan's charges and the applicable calculation rules. If no additional finance charges are included, APR may be close to the interest rate.

Does APR include every fee?

No. Regulation Z defines which charges are finance charges and includes specific exclusions. Some charges, such as certain application fees or actual late-payment charges, may be excluded. Review the disclosed APR and the separate fee schedule.

Can a personal loan have a 0% interest rate but a cost?

A loan advertised with no interest could still have charges or conditions that affect its cost. Read the agreement and disclosures carefully and compare the APR, fees, amount received and total payments.

Can APR tell me which loan has the lower monthly payment?

No. The monthly payment depends on the principal, interest rate, term and repayment structure. Compare the disclosed payment as well as APR and total repayment cost.

Why is my APR higher than the rate in an advertisement?

The advertised rate may be a lowest-available or conditional rate, while the actual offer reflects your application and any finance charges included in APR. Compare the rate and APR in your written offer and ask the lender to explain the difference.

Should I choose the loan with the lowest APR?

Not automatically. Compare the same net amount and term, total payments, fees, payment affordability, collateral requirements and any variable-rate risk. A low APR does not make a loan suitable if the payment is unaffordable or the terms do not meet your needs.

Does a longer term lower APR?

Not necessarily. A longer term may lower the monthly payment but does not automatically reduce APR. It can increase total interest paid because repayment lasts longer.

Where can I verify the APR and finance charges?

Review the lender's Truth in Lending disclosures, loan agreement and fee schedule. Ask the lender to identify the finance charges included in the APR and the fees that are separate from it.

PMI BASICS

What is private mortgage insurance?

Private mortgage insurance, commonly called PMI, is insurance that can protect a mortgage lender if a borrower stops making payments. PMI is different from homeowners insurance, which protects against covered property losses and is a separate cost.

For many conventional mortgages, PMI may be required when the borrower's down payment is less than 20% of the home's purchase price. The exact requirement, cost and rules depend on the mortgage and lender.

Why does PMI exist?

A smaller down payment generally means the borrower starts with less equity in the home. PMI can reduce some of the lender's risk associated with a conventional mortgage that has a higher loan-to-value ratio.

PMI does not protect the borrower from losing the home or erase the borrower's obligation to repay the mortgage.

How does PMI affect monthly housing costs?

PMI can be included in the monthly amount a borrower pays to the mortgage servicer. Depending on the loan, it may be collected monthly or structured in another permitted way.

When comparing home-buying scenarios, consider principal and interest together with property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and other recurring housing costs. A mortgage payment calculator that excludes PMI can therefore understate the full monthly housing budget for a particular scenario.

How is PMI related to the down payment?

Down payment is the portion of the purchase price paid upfront rather than financed through the mortgage. For example, on a hypothetical $400,000 home, a 10% down payment would be $40,000 and the starting mortgage would be $360,000 before considering other transaction costs.

That example is purely illustrative. A larger down payment can reduce the starting loan balance and may affect whether PMI is required, but buyers should compare the cash required upfront with their broader financial circumstances and reserves.

Does every mortgage have PMI?

No. PMI is associated with private mortgage insurance on eligible conventional mortgages; other mortgage programs can have different forms of mortgage insurance or guarantee-related costs. For example, FHA loans have mortgage insurance requirements that differ from conventional PMI, while VA loans generally do not use conventional PMI.

The name, structure, duration and cost of mortgage insurance depend on the loan program and its rules. Always confirm the specific requirements for the mortgage you are considering.

When can PMI end?

PMI cancellation and termination rules depend on the mortgage, applicable law and the borrower's circumstances. For many conventional mortgages, federal rules provide protections concerning cancellation or automatic termination of borrower-paid PMI when specified conditions are met.

Because individual loan documents and payment histories matter, borrowers should review their mortgage disclosures and contact their servicer to determine when and how PMI can be cancelled or terminated for their loan.

PMI vs. homeowners insurance

CostGeneral purpose
PMIPrivate mortgage insurance can protect the lender against certain losses associated with a conventional mortgage.
Homeowners insuranceInsurance coverage can protect the property and other insured interests against covered risks under the policy.

These are different products with different purposes. Having homeowners insurance does not replace PMI when PMI is required.

How can you compare a PMI scenario?

Start with the purchase price, down payment, mortgage amount, interest rate and loan term. Then account for estimated PMI, property taxes and homeowners insurance to get a broader picture of recurring housing costs.

When evaluating a real mortgage, use the lender's disclosures for the actual PMI amount and terms. The PMI Calculator is useful for exploring assumptions, not for replacing a lender's quote.

Questions to ask your lender

  • Is PMI required for this specific loan?
  • How much will the PMI cost under the quoted terms?
  • How will PMI be collected?
  • What are the applicable cancellation or termination rules?
  • What happens to the monthly payment if PMI ends?
  • How does a larger or smaller down payment change the loan terms and total cash needed?

PMI and affordability

A home can appear affordable when looking only at principal and interest, yet become more expensive after taxes, insurance and PMI are included. That is why a broader housing-cost estimate is useful when researching a home price.

Frequently asked questions

Is PMI the same as mortgage insurance?

PMI specifically refers to private mortgage insurance, generally used with conventional mortgages. Other loan programs can have different mortgage-insurance structures and terminology.

Does PMI protect me if I cannot pay my mortgage?

No. PMI is primarily protection for the lender against certain losses. It does not remove the borrower's obligation to make mortgage payments.

Can I avoid PMI with a larger down payment?

For many conventional mortgages, a down payment of at least 20% can avoid borrower-paid PMI, but loan requirements vary. Confirm the specific terms with the lender.

Can PMI be cancelled?

It may be cancellable or automatically terminable under applicable rules and the mortgage's terms. Contact the mortgage servicer for the requirements that apply to your loan.

Should I put more money down just to avoid PMI?

There is no universal answer. Compare the cash required upfront, the resulting loan amount and payment, PMI cost, available reserves and the other costs of the transaction.

HOW IT WORKS

How a mortgage points calculator works

Mortgage discount points are upfront charges paid to the lender in exchange for a lower interest rate. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says one point generally equals 1% of the loan amount, but the amount by which a point reduces the rate depends on the lender, loan type and market conditions.

This calculator estimates the cost of the entered points, calculates principal-and-interest payments at both rates, and divides the upfront points cost by the monthly payment savings to produce a simple break-even estimate.

The break-even figure is only a basic comparison. It does not account for taxes, insurance, refinancing, selling the home, the time value of money, tax treatment, lender-specific fees or changes to the loan balance.

EXAMPLE

Mortgage points example

Suppose you borrow $300,000 for 30 years. If you pay 1 point, the estimated upfront cost is $3,000. If the rate falls from 6.50% to 6.25%, the calculator compares the resulting principal-and-interest payments and estimates how many months of payment savings would equal the $3,000 upfront cost.

Use the actual rate-and-points options shown by your lender or on your Loan Estimate when making a real comparison.

FAQ

Mortgage points calculator FAQs

What are mortgage points?

Discount points are upfront charges paid to a lender to obtain a lower interest rate than you would otherwise receive.

How much is one point?

One discount point generally equals 1% of the loan amount. On a $300,000 mortgage, one point would be $3,000.

Does one point equal a 0.25% rate reduction?

No. The rate reduction is not fixed. It depends on the lender, loan product and market conditions.

What is the break-even period?

It is a simple estimate of how long the monthly payment savings would take to equal the upfront cost of the points.

Should I compare points using total interest?

Total interest can be useful, but it assumes you keep the loan for the full term. Your expected time in the home or mortgage is also important when comparing upfront costs and monthly savings.

Are taxes and insurance included?

No. The comparison focuses on principal and interest. Other housing and closing costs are excluded.

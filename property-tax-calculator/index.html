PROPERTY TAX GUIDE

How this property tax calculator works

The calculator starts with the assessed value you enter and subtracts the eligible dollar exemption. It then applies the combined local rate to the taxable assessed value. The rate can be entered as a percentage or as a mill rate per $1,000 of assessed value.

  • Taxable assessed value: assessed value − exemption applied (not below zero).
  • Percent-rate method: taxable assessed value × annual rate percentage ÷ 100.
  • Mill-rate method: taxable assessed value ÷ 1,000 × mill rate.
  • Monthly estimate: estimated annual tax ÷ 12.

One mill represents $1 of tax per $1,000 of taxable assessed value. For example, 20 mills equals 2.00% of taxable assessed value. The rate should represent the combined rate for all applicable taxing districts, such as county, city and school district, where those rates are separately listed.

The separate market or purchase value input is used to show the assessed-value ratio and the effective tax rate relative to market value. It does not replace the assessed value used in the tax calculation.

Market value vs. assessed value

Market value is an estimate of what the property may sell for. Assessed value is the figure used by the local property-tax system, which may be a percentage of market value or may be adjusted under local valuation rules. For this calculator, enter the assessed value shown on the current assessment notice; do not automatically substitute the purchase price.

The assessment ratio shown in the results is simply assessed value divided by the market or purchase value you entered. It is an observation about your inputs, not a state-specific assessment rule or an automatically applied assessment factor.

Why property taxes can change

Your property tax bill can change when assessed values, local tax levies or rates, exemptions, credits, or special assessments change. The combined tax rate may include several taxing authorities. Your actual bill may also list separate fees or assessments that are not included in this simplified rate calculation. Check the current tax statement and assessor records for the property.

Property tax and mortgage escrow

Property taxes are often collected through mortgage escrow. When taxes are escrowed, the mortgage servicer collects a monthly amount and pays the local tax bill when due. Because property taxes and insurance can change, the escrow portion of a monthly mortgage payment can change too. If taxes are not escrowed, the homeowner must budget for the local billing schedule directly.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Example property tax calculation

Suppose a home has a $400,000 assessed value, a combined annual rate of 1.2%, and no exemption. Estimated annual property tax is $4,800, or $400 per month. The same calculation can be entered as a 12-mill rate, because 12 mills equals $12 per $1,000 of assessed value.

If the same property has a $50,000 exemption, taxable assessed value becomes $350,000. At 1.2% (or 12 mills), estimated annual tax is $4,200, or $350 per month.

These examples illustrate the arithmetic only. Local systems may apply assessment ratios, equalization factors, multiple taxing districts, credits, special assessments or other rules that this general calculator does not model.

US TAX CONTEXT

Property tax deductions

Federal tax treatment is separate from the property-tax calculation itself. IRS rules can allow certain taxpayers who itemize deductions to deduct qualifying state and local taxes, subject to applicable limitations. This calculator does not estimate a federal or state tax deduction.

For current tax treatment, use official IRS guidance or consult a qualified tax professional rather than relying on the calculator's property-tax estimate.

FAQ

US property tax calculator FAQs

What value should I use for property tax?

Use the assessed value used by your local taxing authority when it is available. If you only know the home's market or purchase value, treat the result as a rough estimate unless your local jurisdiction uses that value directly.

What property tax rate should I enter?

Enter the combined annual rate for the property's applicable taxing districts. If the figures are listed separately for a county, city and school district, add the applicable rates if the local instructions say they should be combined. You can enter the rate as a percentage or select mill rate and enter dollars per $1,000 of taxable assessment.

Does this calculator include exemptions?

Yes. Enter the eligible dollar exemption and the calculator subtracts it from assessed value before applying the rate. If the exemption exceeds the assessment, taxable value is set to zero and the unused amount is shown separately. Eligibility and amounts depend on local rules.

What is the difference between assessed value and market value?

Market value reflects an estimate of what the property could sell for, while assessed value is the value used under the local property-tax system. They can be equal, but they do not have to be.

Does property tax affect my mortgage payment?

It can. When property taxes are paid through escrow, the estimated monthly tax amount is collected with the mortgage payment. Changes in taxes or insurance can change the escrow portion.

Can I use this calculator for any US state?

You can use it as a general arithmetic estimator if you have the taxable assessed value and combined rate. State and local systems differ: some use assessment ratios or equalization factors, multiple taxing authorities, credits and special assessments. This tool does not calculate those jurisdiction-specific steps automatically.

Are property taxes deductible on federal taxes?

Some taxpayers who itemize may be able to deduct qualifying state and local taxes subject to current federal rules and limitations. This calculator does not calculate a tax deduction.

Is this property tax calculator tax advice?

No. It is an educational estimate. Check your local tax authority for the actual assessment, rate, exemptions and bill, and consult a qualified tax professional for tax questions.

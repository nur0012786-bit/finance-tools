PROPERTY TAX GUIDE

How this property tax calculator works

The calculator starts with the assessed value you enter and subtracts the annual exemption. The remaining amount is treated as the taxable assessed value. The calculator then multiplies that taxable value by your entered annual property tax rate.

Formula: taxable assessed value = assessed value − exemption. Estimated annual tax = taxable assessed value × tax rate. Estimated monthly tax = annual tax ÷ 12.

The separate property-value input lets the calculator show an effective tax rate relative to the property's stated value. This is useful when market or purchase value differs from assessed value.

Market value vs. assessed value

A home's market value and assessed value are not necessarily the same. Local taxing authorities determine assessments under state and local rules, and assessment ratios or reassessment schedules can differ. If you have a current assessment notice or tax record, use that assessed value instead of assuming the purchase price is taxable.

Why property taxes can change

Your property tax bill can change when the assessed value changes, a local tax rate changes, an exemption changes, or special assessments are added. A purchase, reassessment, improvement or change in local rules can therefore make an older tax bill a poor estimate of future taxes.

Property tax and mortgage escrow

Property taxes are often collected through mortgage escrow. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that escrow allows property-related expenses such as taxes and insurance to be paid through monthly amounts collected with the mortgage. Because taxes and insurance can change, the escrow portion of a mortgage payment can change as well.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Example property tax calculation

Suppose a home has a $400,000 assessed value, a 1.2% annual property tax rate and no exemption. The estimated annual property tax is $4,800, or $400 per month.

If the same property had a $50,000 exemption, the taxable assessed value would be $350,000. At the same 1.2% rate, estimated annual tax would be $4,200, or $350 per month.

These examples illustrate the calculator's arithmetic only. Your local tax authority may use assessment ratios, mill rates, multiple taxing districts, special assessments or other rules that are not represented here.

US TAX CONTEXT

Property tax deductions

Federal tax treatment is separate from the property-tax calculation itself. IRS rules can allow certain taxpayers who itemize deductions to deduct qualifying state and local taxes, subject to applicable limitations. This calculator does not estimate a federal or state tax deduction.

For current tax treatment, use official IRS guidance or consult a qualified tax professional rather than relying on the calculator's property-tax estimate.

FAQ

US property tax calculator FAQs

What value should I use for property tax?

Use the assessed value used by your local taxing authority when it is available. If you only know the home's market or purchase value, treat the result as a rough estimate unless your local jurisdiction uses that value directly.

What property tax rate should I enter?

Enter the annual rate that applies to the property and jurisdiction. If your local authority publishes a mill rate or a different assessment formula, convert it appropriately or use the tax amount from your local records instead.

Does this calculator include exemptions?

Yes. Enter an annual exemption amount and the calculator subtracts it from assessed value before applying the tax rate. Eligibility and exemption amounts depend on local rules.

What is the difference between assessed value and market value?

Market value reflects an estimate of what the property could sell for, while assessed value is the value used under the local property-tax system. They can be equal, but they do not have to be.

Does property tax affect my mortgage payment?

It can. When property taxes are paid through escrow, the estimated monthly tax amount is collected with the mortgage payment. Changes in taxes or insurance can change the escrow portion.

Can I use this calculator for any US state?

You can use it as a general arithmetic estimator, but local property-tax systems differ. Some jurisdictions use assessment ratios, mill rates, multiple taxing authorities, exemptions and special assessments that this simple calculator does not model separately.

Are property taxes deductible on federal taxes?

Some taxpayers who itemize may be able to deduct qualifying state and local taxes subject to current federal rules and limitations. This calculator does not calculate a tax deduction.

Is this property tax calculator tax advice?

No. It is an educational estimate. Check your local tax authority for the actual assessment, rate, exemptions and bill, and consult a qualified tax professional for tax questions.

US MORTGAGE GUIDE

How Mortgage Payments Are Calculated

A practical guide to estimating a US mortgage payment, understanding principal and interest, and accounting for taxes, homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance.

What makes up a mortgage payment?

A mortgage payment can include more than principal and interest. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) explains that the total monthly payment may include principal, interest, mortgage insurance and escrowed property taxes and homeowners insurance.

For a quick principal-and-interest estimate, use the Mortgage Calculator. For a broader affordability estimate, use the Mortgage Affordability Calculator.

Principal and interest

Principal is the amount borrowed. Each principal payment reduces the outstanding balance. Interest is the lender's charge for providing the loan. With a typical fixed-rate mortgage, the scheduled principal-and-interest payment is designed to pay the loan down over the selected term.

The monthly payment formula

For a fixed-rate loan, the principal-and-interest payment can be estimated with:

M = P × [r(1+r)n] ÷ [(1+r)n − 1]

  • M = monthly principal-and-interest payment
  • P = loan principal
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = total number of monthly payments

The annual rate is converted to a monthly rate for this calculation, and a 30-year mortgage has 360 scheduled monthly payments.

Why the payment changes when the loan changes

A larger loan generally produces a larger principal-and-interest payment when the rate and term are unchanged. A higher interest rate also increases the payment. A longer term can lower the scheduled monthly principal-and-interest amount, but it can increase the total interest paid over the full repayment period.

Taxes, insurance and PMI

Property taxes and homeowners insurance are costs of owning the home. They may be collected with the mortgage payment through an escrow account, depending on the loan and arrangement. Mortgage insurance may also be part of the total payment when required.

Because these costs vary by property, location and loan structure, a calculator that shows only principal and interest should not be treated as the complete monthly housing cost.

Worked example

Suppose a borrower finances $300,000 with a fixed 30-year term at 6.5% annual interest. The principal-and-interest calculation produces an estimated monthly payment of about $1,896. The actual total housing payment can be higher after adding applicable property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and other costs.

Down payment and loan amount

A down payment reduces the amount financed. For example, a $400,000 home with $80,000 down has a $320,000 starting loan amount before other financing adjustments. A larger down payment can therefore reduce the principal-and-interest payment, although the overall financial effect depends on the borrower's rate, cash reserves, mortgage insurance and other costs.

Use the Down Payment Calculator to compare cash requirements and the Mortgage Affordability Calculator to explore a broader budget scenario.

What to check when comparing mortgage offers

Don't compare offers using the monthly principal-and-interest payment alone. The CFPB recommends reviewing the Loan Estimate, including the loan amount, interest rate, total monthly payment, mortgage insurance when applicable, upfront loan costs and cash to close.

Points and lender credits can also change the tradeoff between upfront costs and the interest rate. The effect depends on the lender, loan and market conditions, so compare the actual Loan Estimates you receive.

Mortgage payment FAQ

Does a mortgage payment include property taxes?

It can. Property taxes may be included in an escrow portion of the payment, or the homeowner may pay them separately. Check the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure for the specific loan.

Does a mortgage payment include homeowners insurance?

It can. Homeowners insurance is commonly included in escrow when an escrow account is used, but arrangements vary.

Is the mortgage calculator's payment my full housing cost?

Not necessarily. Principal and interest are only part of the cost. Taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and other ownership expenses may need to be considered separately.

Why does a 30-year mortgage usually have a lower payment than a 15-year mortgage?

The balance is spread across more scheduled payments. This generally lowers the scheduled monthly principal-and-interest payment, but the longer repayment period can result in more total interest if the loan is held to maturity.

How PMI works

Private mortgage insurance is commonly associated with conventional mortgages when the borrower's equity is below a lender's required level. PMI protects the lender against certain losses; it does not protect the homeowner from missed payments.

PMI estimate

This calculator estimates annual PMI by multiplying the mortgage balance by your entered annual PMI rate. It then divides that estimate by 12 for an estimated monthly PMI cost.

Why your down payment matters

A larger down payment reduces the mortgage balance and lowers the starting LTV. On conventional loans, reaching 20% equity can often eliminate the need for borrower-paid PMI at origination, subject to lender and loan rules.

PMI is not the same as FHA mortgage insurance

Conventional PMI and FHA mortgage insurance operate under different program rules. If you are comparing loan programs, review the specific insurance, upfront costs and ongoing requirements that apply to each loan.

PMI FAQs

What is PMI?

PMI is private mortgage insurance commonly used with conventional mortgages when required by the loan structure.

How is PMI estimated?

A simple estimate is mortgage balance × annual PMI rate ÷ 12.

When is PMI required?

Many conventional loans require PMI when the down payment is below 20%, but requirements vary.

Can I avoid PMI with 20% down?

A 20% down payment can often avoid borrower-paid PMI on a conventional mortgage, subject to loan and lender rules.

Can PMI be removed?

Eligible conventional borrowers may have rights to request or obtain cancellation when certain equity conditions are met.

Is this a PMI quote?

No. It is an estimate based on the inputs you provide.

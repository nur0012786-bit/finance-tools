How this US PMI calculator works

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) is generally associated with conventional mortgages when the down payment is below 20%. PMI protects the lender rather than the homeowner. The exact premium depends on the loan and borrower, so this calculator asks you to enter an estimated annual PMI rate instead of assuming one universal rate.

PMI calculation methodology

The calculator first subtracts your down payment from the home price to estimate the mortgage balance. Starting LTV is then calculated as mortgage balance divided by home price. For the PMI estimate, the mortgage balance is multiplied by your entered annual PMI rate and divided by 12:

Estimated monthly PMI = loan amount × annual PMI rate ÷ 12

The page also calculates principal and interest using the entered rate and term, then adds estimated monthly PMI to show a combined principal-and-interest-plus-PMI figure. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA dues, and other housing costs are not included in that result.

Why the PMI rate is an input

PMI pricing is not a single fixed percentage. It can vary with factors such as down payment, credit score, loan amount, and mortgage type. Fannie Mae notes that PMI is commonly priced as a percentage of the mortgage amount.

When PMI may end

For many eligible conventional mortgages covered by the federal PMI rules, a borrower can request cancellation when the principal balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, subject to applicable conditions. In general, automatic termination occurs when the scheduled balance reaches 78% of original value if the borrower is current. FHA and VA mortgage-insurance rules are different.

PMI is not the same as FHA mortgage insurance

Conventional PMI and government-loan mortgage insurance follow different program rules. FHA loans have their own mortgage-insurance requirements, while VA loans have different fee and insurance structures. Use the dedicated FHA and VA calculators when comparing those programs.

PMI calculator FAQs

What is PMI?

PMI is private mortgage insurance that is commonly required on conventional mortgages with smaller down payments. It protects the lender, not the borrower.

How much does PMI cost?

There is no universal PMI rate. The premium can vary based on factors including down payment, credit profile, loan amount, and mortgage type. Enter an estimated annual rate to model your own scenario.

Can I avoid PMI with 20% down?

For a conventional mortgage, a 20% down payment generally means borrower-paid PMI is not required at origination, although specific loan and lender terms apply.

When can PMI be canceled?

For many eligible conventional mortgages, you can request cancellation when the principal balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the home's original value if the applicable requirements are met. Automatic termination generally occurs at 78% when the borrower is current.

Does FHA use PMI?

No. FHA uses its own mortgage-insurance rules. This calculator is designed for an estimated conventional PMI scenario, not an FHA mortgage-insurance calculation.

Is this a PMI quote?

No. It is a mathematical estimate using your home price, down payment, mortgage rate, term, and assumed PMI rate. A lender or mortgage insurer may quote a different premium.

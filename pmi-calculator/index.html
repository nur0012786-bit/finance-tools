How this PMI calculator works

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) is generally associated with conventional mortgages when the down payment is less than 20%. PMI protects the lender—not the homeowner—against loss if the borrower defaults. FHA, VA and USDA mortgage-insurance or guarantee-fee rules are different and are not calculated by this tool. Read Fannie Mae's overview of private mortgage insurance.

1. Calculate the starting loan and LTV

The estimated base loan is the original home value minus the down payment. Starting loan-to-value is the loan amount divided by the original home value:

Starting LTV = loan amount ÷ original home value × 100

For a purchase, the Homeowners Protection Act generally defines original value as the lower of the contract sales price or the appraised value at purchase. For a refinance, it generally means the appraised value at the time of refinance. Use the figure that applies to your loan documents.

2. Estimate the PMI premium

This calculator uses the annual PMI rate you enter as a percentage of the original loan amount:

Estimated monthly PMI = loan amount × annual PMI rate ÷ 12

There is no single PMI rate for every borrower. Fannie Mae identifies down payment, credit score, loan amount and mortgage type as factors that affect the premium. Its homebuyer FAQ describes a broad annual range of 0.2% to 2% of the loan amount; your own quote may differ. Use your Loan Estimate or insurer quote when available.

If the starting LTV is 80% or lower, the calculator assumes borrower-paid monthly PMI is not required at origination for a typical conventional purchase mortgage. Loan terms and lender-paid or single-premium arrangements can differ.

3. Estimate the mortgage payment

Principal and interest use the standard fixed-rate mortgage amortization formula. The estimated monthly housing total shown here adds principal and interest, the entered PMI estimate, estimated property tax and homeowners insurance. It does not include HOA dues, utilities, maintenance, closing costs or other charges.

4. Estimate when the scheduled balance reaches 80% and 78%

The calculator simulates the original fixed-rate amortization schedule with regular on-time payments and no additional principal payments. It identifies the scheduled month when the balance first falls to 80% of original value and the month when it first falls to 78%.

For many conventional mortgages covered by the federal Homeowners Protection Act (HPA), a borrower may request PMI cancellation at the scheduled 80% point, or earlier if additional principal payments bring the actual balance to 80%. A written request, good payment history, current payments, no junior lien and evidence that the property value has not declined may be required. The servicer's PMI disclosure and loan-specific terms control the process.

For many covered loans, PMI generally must automatically terminate at the scheduled 78% point if the borrower is current. If the balance has not reached 78%, the HPA also provides for termination after the midpoint of the original amortization schedule, provided the borrower is current. The calculator shows an indicative month for this rule; it cannot confirm HPA coverage, high-risk-loan exceptions or eligibility.

These federal requirements generally apply to covered single-family principal-residence mortgages closed on or after July 29, 1999. FHA and VA loans, lender-paid mortgage insurance and certain other transactions follow different rules. Review the CFPB's PMI cancellation guidance and contact your servicer for a decision.

What can change your PMI cost?

PMI pricing is risk-based rather than a universal flat rate. Fannie Mae identifies factors such as down payment, credit score, mortgage amount and loan type. Different insurers and lenders can also offer different premium structures, including monthly, single-premium or lender-paid arrangements. Ask the lender to compare the total cost and terms—not only the monthly PMI line item.

Why the annual PMI rate is an input

This calculator does not have access to your credit report, loan-level pricing, insurer rate card or lender quote. It therefore asks you to enter an assumed annual PMI rate. The 0.70% prefilled value is an example input, not a universal average or recommended quote.

PMI is not FHA mortgage insurance

Conventional PMI and FHA mortgage insurance have different cancellation rules and premium structures. FHA annual MIP may continue for the mortgage term depending on the original LTV and case rules. Use the FHA Mortgage Calculator for a standard FHA purchase estimate, not this PMI tool.

PMI calculator FAQs

What is PMI?

Private mortgage insurance is commonly required on conventional mortgages with less than 20% down. It protects the lender, not the homeowner, against losses if the borrower defaults.

How much does PMI cost per month?

It depends on the loan and borrower. This calculator estimates monthly PMI by applying the annual rate you enter to the original loan amount and dividing by 12. The actual lender or insurer quote may be different.

Can I avoid PMI with 20% down?

For a typical borrower-paid PMI conventional mortgage, a down payment of 20% or more generally means PMI is not required at origination. Different lender-paid or special loan structures may have different pricing.

When can I request PMI cancellation at 80% LTV?

For many HPA-covered mortgages, you may submit a written cancellation request when the scheduled balance reaches 80% of original value, or earlier if extra payments bring the actual balance to that level. You must meet applicable payment-history, current-payment, junior-lien and property-value conditions.

Does PMI automatically end at 78% LTV?

For many covered mortgages, the servicer generally must automatically terminate PMI when the scheduled balance reaches 78% of original value if payments are current. If 78% is not reached, the HPA midpoint rule may apply to covered loans.

What is the original value of the home?

For a purchase mortgage, it generally means the lower of the contract sales price or the appraised value at purchase. For a refinance, it generally means the appraised value at the time of refinance.

Does paying extra principal remove PMI sooner?

Extra principal can bring the actual balance to 80% of original value before the scheduled date, which may let you request cancellation earlier if you meet the servicer's conditions. This calculator's milestone schedule assumes regular payments only and does not model extra payments.

Is this calculator valid for FHA or VA mortgage insurance?

No. FHA, VA and USDA programs have different fees and insurance rules. This calculator is intended for a simplified borrower-paid conventional PMI estimate.

Is this an official PMI quote or cancellation decision?

No. The calculator provides a mathematical estimate. Your PMI disclosure, mortgage documents, investor rules and servicer determine the premium and whether cancellation or termination requirements are met.

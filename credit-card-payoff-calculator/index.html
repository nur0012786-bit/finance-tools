How to use this credit card payoff calculator

Enter the balance you want to repay, the card's APR, the fixed amount you plan to pay each month, and any additional amount you can add. The calculator compares two scenarios: your regular payment and your regular payment plus the extra amount.

  • Credit card balance: Enter the balance that is currently accruing interest.
  • APR: Enter the annual percentage rate that applies to that balance, as a percentage. For example, enter 24 for 24%.
  • Monthly payment: Enter the total fixed amount you plan to pay toward this card each month. This is not automatically the minimum payment shown on your statement.
  • Extra monthly payment: Enter an additional amount on top of the regular payment. Enter zero if you do not plan to pay extra.

Click Calculate payoff. The results show the estimated time and interest for both payment scenarios, the difference between them, and the month-by-month schedule for the scenario with the extra payment.

How the payoff calculation works

This tool uses a simplified monthly-interest model. For each month, it estimates interest as the current balance multiplied by APR divided by 12, adds that interest to the balance, and applies your fixed payment. Interest and balances are rounded to cents during the simulation. The final payment is reduced to the amount still owed.

The model assumes the APR and monthly payment stay constant, that no new purchases or fees are added, and that each payment is made in time for the modeled monthly cycle. It does not calculate an issuer's actual statement or minimum payment. The payoff date is a planning estimate based on the current date and the modeled number of monthly payments.

Example: paying an extra $50 each month

Suppose the card balance is $5,000, the APR is 24%, and your regular monthly payment is $200. Under this calculator's monthly model, the regular-payment scenario takes about 36 months and costs about $2,000.57 in interest.

Adding $50 each month raises the modeled payment to $250. The estimated payoff time falls to about 26 months, with about $1,449.37 in interest. In this illustration, the extra $50 saves about 10 months and $551.20 in interest. Your own result will change with the balance, APR, payment timing and card terms.

Understand the results

  • Payoff time: The estimated number of monthly payments until the balance reaches zero with the extra payment included.
  • Total interest: The sum of modeled monthly interest charges. It is not a quoted amount from your issuer.
  • Total paid: The modeled payments, including interest and principal, needed to clear the balance.
  • Debt-free date: An estimate based on the current date and the number of modeled months. Actual payment posting dates can move it.
  • Months and interest saved: The difference between the regular-payment scenario and the extra-payment scenario. These comparisons are shown only when both scenarios pay off within the model's limit.
  • Payoff schedule: The estimated payment, interest, principal reduction and remaining balance for each month in the extra-payment scenario.

Why your credit card statement may show different interest

Many card issuers calculate interest daily using a daily periodic rate and daily or average daily balances. The daily periodic rate may be based on APR divided by 360 or 365, depending on the issuer's method. Because purchases, payments and credits can change the balance during a billing cycle, the actual interest on your statement can differ from a monthly APR-divided-by-12 estimate. Read the CFPB's explanation of how credit card interest is calculated and daily periodic rates.

Cards with different APRs or promotional balances

This calculator models one balance at one APR. Your statement may separate purchases, balance transfers, cash advances and promotional balances because different APRs or terms can apply. To estimate one of those categories, use the balance and APR for that category rather than combining balances with different rates.

When a payment is greater than the required minimum, federal rules generally require the amount above the minimum to go first to the balance with the highest APR. The issuer generally has more flexibility in allocating the minimum-payment portion. Special rules can apply to deferred-interest promotional balances. See Regulation Z, § 1026.53. This calculator does not model that allocation across multiple balance categories.

What this calculator does not include

  • New purchases, cash advances, balance transfers or other transactions added after the starting balance.
  • Late fees, annual fees, balance-transfer fees, cash-advance fees or other charges.
  • Variable APR changes, penalty APRs, promotional-rate expiration or deferred-interest terms.
  • Daily transaction-level interest, grace-period eligibility or an issuer's exact payment-posting rules.
  • Issuer minimum-payment formulas, payment allocation across multiple APR categories or multiple credit cards.

If you carry multiple cards, run a separate estimate for each one, using its own balance, APR and planned payment. Keep making at least each required minimum by its due date. If you cannot afford a minimum payment, contact the issuer promptly and ask about hardship options; the CFPB offers guidance on what to do when you cannot pay your credit card bills.

Frequently asked questions

Is the monthly payment field the same as my minimum payment?

No. Enter the fixed amount you plan to pay each month. Your issuer sets the actual minimum payment, which can change from one statement to the next. Always pay at least the amount and by the due date shown on your statement.

Does this calculator use daily credit card interest?

No. It estimates interest monthly using APR divided by 12 and rounds interest to cents each modeled month. Many issuers calculate interest daily, so your statement can differ.

What if my regular payment does not cover monthly interest?

The regular-payment scenario is shown as not paying off within the model. If the extra payment is large enough to reduce the balance, the calculator can still show that separate scenario. Increase the payment amount or contact your issuer if you are struggling to keep up.

Can I enter a 0% APR?

Yes. The calculator can model a zero-interest balance, provided the monthly payment is greater than zero. Check whether your promotional rate has an expiration date and what APR applies afterward.

Why does the final payment differ from my regular payment?

The calculator caps the final payment at the remaining balance plus the modeled interest. You do not need to make a full fixed payment after the balance is paid off.

Will the payoff date match my issuer's date?

Not necessarily. It is estimated from the current date and the number of modeled monthly payments. Actual statement dates, payment posting, weekends, holidays and issuer processing can change the date.

Can I use the calculator for several cards?

Use it separately for each card, because each can have a different APR, balance and due date. This single-card calculator does not combine multiple accounts or allocate one payment across them.

Does paying more than the minimum reduce interest?

Paying more generally reduces the balance faster and can lower future interest. On accounts with multiple APR balances, federal payment-allocation rules generally direct the amount above the minimum to the highest-rate balance first, subject to exceptions.

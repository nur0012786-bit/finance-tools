Personal Loan Fees Explained: Common Costs to Check

Learn about common personal loan fees, including origination charges, late fees and other costs that can affect borrowing.

Educational finance guidance

Personal loan fees vary by lender and loan. Reviewing the complete fee schedule is important because the rate alone does not describe the total cost of borrowing.

Origination fees

An origination fee is a charge some lenders apply for processing or originating a loan. The amount and how it is collected can vary.

Late-payment fees

A loan agreement may specify a fee or other consequence for late payments. Review the grace period and late-payment terms before accepting a loan.

Other possible charges

Depending on the product, there may be application-related charges, returned-payment fees or other contract-specific costs. Do not assume a fee applies unless it is disclosed in the agreement.

Fees and APR

Certain finance charges can be reflected in APR under applicable rules, which is one reason APR can be more informative than an interest rate alone when comparing similar offers.

Illustrative example

A hypothetical loan with a lower rate but a large upfront fee may have a different borrowing cost from a loan with a slightly higher rate and fewer fees. Compare the full disclosed terms.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

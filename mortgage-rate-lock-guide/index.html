What Is a Mortgage Rate Lock?

Understand mortgage rate locks, lock periods, extensions, float-down options and what can happen when a mortgage rate lock expires.

Educational mortgage guidance

A mortgage rate lock is an agreement under which a lender holds a specified interest rate for a stated period, subject to the terms and conditions of the lock.

UNDERSTAND THE BASICS

What you should know

A lock can provide protection against rate changes during the stated period, but it does not necessarily guarantee every loan term or prevent all changes caused by changes in the application or transaction.

Key comparison points

Ask how long the lock lasts, whether there is a fee, what happens if closing is delayed, whether extensions are available, and whether a float-down option exists.

Illustrative scenario

A lock can expire before closing. The consequences depend on the lender's agreement, so borrowers should understand the expiration date and extension rules before relying on the quoted rate.

This example is educational only. Actual mortgage terms, costs and eligibility depend on the loan, lender, property and borrower circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

Are mortgage rules the same for every borrower?

No. Loan-program requirements, lender policies, property characteristics and borrower circumstances can affect mortgage terms and eligibility.

Can a calculator guarantee what a lender will approve?

No. Calculators are educational tools. A lender uses its own underwriting rules, documentation and loan-program requirements.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual mortgage?

Review the lender's official disclosures and loan documents and ask the lender or servicer about any item you do not understand.

HOW IT WORKS

How the down payment calculator works

The calculator multiplies the purchase price by the down payment percentage you enter. That produces the estimated upfront down payment; subtracting it from the purchase price gives the estimated mortgage amount before other transaction adjustments.

It then adds your estimated closing costs to the down payment to calculate cash needed before deposits. Earnest money already paid is treated as a credit toward that amount, so the calculator estimates the additional cash still needed. Any cash left over is calculated from the available-cash figure you enter.

This page does not decide what down payment is appropriate for you. It is a budgeting model that helps you compare upfront cash requirements with the amount you would need to finance.

US HOME-BUYING GUIDANCE

Down payment is only part of your upfront budget

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends considering your available savings, other financial goals, moving costs, renovations and an emergency cushion before deciding how much cash to put toward a down payment. It also notes that closing costs are separate from the down payment.

Down payment requirements vary by loan type and lender. The CFPB notes that some conventional loans can have low down payment options, while FHA, VA and USDA programs have their own eligibility and financing rules.

EXAMPLE

Example down payment comparison

On a $400,000 home, a 10% down payment is $40,000, leaving an estimated $360,000 mortgage. At 20%, the down payment is $80,000 and the estimated mortgage is $320,000. If closing costs were $12,000, the corresponding cash needed before deposits would be $52,000 and $92,000.

For example, with $100,000 available for closing and $5,000 of earnest money already paid, the 20% scenario would leave about $13,000 after the remaining estimated cash requirement ($80,000 + $12,000 − $5,000 = $87,000). This is a simple budgeting example; actual cash-to-close can also reflect lender credits, seller credits, prepaid items, deposits, and other transaction adjustments.

FAQ

Down payment calculator FAQs

Do I need 20% down?

No. Down payment requirements vary by loan program and lender. Some options allow substantially less than 20% down.

Does a bigger down payment lower the loan amount?

Yes. For a given purchase price, every additional dollar paid upfront generally reduces the amount that must be financed.

Does a down payment include closing costs?

No. Closing costs are separate. Your total cash to close can include the down payment and closing costs, less deposits and credits that apply.

Could a smaller down payment increase monthly costs?

It can. A smaller down payment means a larger loan, and mortgage insurance may also apply depending on the loan and circumstances.

Should I use all my savings for the down payment?

Not necessarily. Your cash budget may also need to cover closing costs, moving or renovation expenses and an emergency reserve. Consider those amounts before treating all available savings as down-payment funds.

Is this calculator a loan-qualification tool?

No. It estimates down payment and cash requirements from the numbers you enter. Lenders use their own underwriting criteria to determine eligibility and loan terms.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not determine loan eligibility or recommend a down payment. Loan programs, lender requirements, mortgage insurance, closing costs and cash-to-close figures can vary.

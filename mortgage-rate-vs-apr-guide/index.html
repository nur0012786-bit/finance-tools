Mortgage Interest Rate vs. APR: What's the Difference?

The mortgage interest rate and annual percentage rate, or APR, are related but different numbers. Understanding what each represents can make mortgage offers easier to compare.

Rate and APR answer different questions

The interest rate is central to calculating interest and principal payments. APR is designed to reflect the interest rate plus certain costs of obtaining the credit, expressed as a yearly rate under prescribed assumptions.

What is a mortgage interest rate?

The mortgage interest rate is the rate used to calculate the interest charged on the outstanding loan balance. For a fixed-rate mortgage, the contractual rate remains fixed according to the loan terms.

The interest rate is a key input when estimating the scheduled principal-and-interest payment. Changing the rate while holding the loan amount and term constant can change both the monthly payment and the total interest paid over the scheduled term.

What is APR?

APR stands for annual percentage rate. It is intended to provide a broader measure of the cost of credit than the stated interest rate by incorporating the interest rate and certain finance charges associated with obtaining the mortgage.

APR is calculated using prescribed assumptions and disclosures. It should not be interpreted as simply another interest rate that will be applied to the outstanding balance each month.

Why is APR often higher than the interest rate?

When a mortgage has certain upfront finance charges, those costs can be reflected in the APR calculation. As a result, the APR may be higher than the stated interest rate.

For example, a hypothetical mortgage could have a 6.50% interest rate and a higher APR because the transaction includes certain finance charges. The exact APR depends on the loan terms, charges and calculation assumptions.

This is an illustration, not a quote or prediction of a particular mortgage offer.

Why the difference matters when comparing mortgages

Two mortgage offers can have similar interest rates but different fees, or different rates and fees that produce different APRs. Looking at both numbers can help reveal that the headline rate does not describe every cost of obtaining the credit.

However, APR should not be used as the only number when comparing offers. Loan amount, term, points, lender credits, cash required at closing, payment, product features and how long you expect to keep the loan can all matter.

How mortgage points affect rate and APR

Discount points are upfront charges paid to the lender in exchange for a lower interest rate under the loan's terms. Paying points can reduce the stated interest rate while increasing upfront costs.

Because certain upfront costs are considered in APR calculations, points can affect the relationship between the stated rate and APR.

Rate, APR and monthly payment

The scheduled principal-and-interest payment is based on the loan amount, interest rate, term and payment structure. APR is not simply substituted for the interest rate in the standard mortgage-payment formula.

That distinction is important: a mortgage can have an APR that is higher than its interest rate while the scheduled payment is still calculated using the stated contractual interest rate.

What should you compare on a Loan Estimate?

When shopping for a mortgage, review the lender's Loan Estimate and compare the complete offer rather than focusing on a single number. The documents provide information about the loan amount, interest rate, projected payments, closing costs and other terms.

For an actual mortgage decision, use the lender's disclosures as the source for the quoted terms and charges.

Example: comparing two hypothetical offers

Imagine two hypothetical 30-year fixed mortgage offers for the same loan amount. Offer A has a lower stated rate but higher upfront finance charges. Offer B has a slightly higher stated rate but lower upfront charges.

Offer A could have the lower monthly principal-and-interest payment, while its APR could reflect the additional finance charges. Offer B could have a higher payment but lower upfront costs. Which structure is preferable depends on the actual terms and the borrower's circumstances and expected time in the loan.

This example demonstrates why rate and APR should be reviewed together rather than treating either number as a complete measure of a mortgage offer.

Rate vs. APR: questions to ask

  • What is the stated interest rate?
  • What is the APR?
  • Which fees and finance charges are included in the APR calculation?
  • Are discount points being paid?
  • What is the total cash needed at closing?
  • What is the scheduled principal-and-interest payment?
  • How would the offer change if points or lender credits changed?
  • What assumptions apply to the quoted APR?

Frequently asked questions

Is APR the same as the mortgage interest rate?

No. The interest rate is the contractual rate used to calculate interest on the loan balance. APR is a broader annualized cost measure that incorporates the rate and certain finance charges under applicable rules.

Why is my APR higher than my interest rate?

One common reason is that the transaction includes finance charges that are reflected in the APR calculation. The exact relationship depends on the mortgage terms and charges.

Does my monthly mortgage payment use the APR?

The scheduled principal-and-interest payment is generally calculated using the contractual interest rate and loan terms, not by simply replacing the interest rate with the APR.

Is a lower APR always a better mortgage?

APR is useful for comparison, but it is not a complete decision rule. Consider the full loan terms, fees, payment, cash required upfront and how long you expect to keep the mortgage.

Where can I find the official APR for a mortgage offer?

Review the lender's required disclosures, including the Loan Estimate for applicable mortgage transactions, and confirm the quoted terms with the lender.

What Is Loan-to-Value Ratio (LTV)?

Learn what mortgage loan-to-value ratio means, how to calculate LTV, and why it can affect mortgage insurance and loan terms.

Educational mortgage guidance

Loan-to-value ratio, or LTV, compares the mortgage balance with the property's value under the applicable lending rules. A common simplified formula is loan amount divided by property value.

UNDERSTAND THE BASICS

What you should know

For a hypothetical $400,000 property with a $320,000 mortgage, the simplified LTV is 80%. The exact value used for lending purposes depends on the loan and transaction.

Key comparison points

LTV can be relevant to mortgage insurance, pricing, eligibility and refinancing considerations. Different loan programs can use different rules.

Illustrative scenario

A larger down payment generally lowers the starting LTV because the financed amount is smaller relative to the purchase price, assuming the same property value.

This example is educational only. Actual mortgage terms, costs and eligibility depend on the loan, lender, property and borrower circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

Are mortgage rules the same for every borrower?

No. Loan-program requirements, lender policies, property characteristics and borrower circumstances can affect mortgage terms and eligibility.

Can a calculator guarantee what a lender will approve?

No. Calculators are educational tools. A lender uses its own underwriting rules, documentation and loan-program requirements.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual mortgage?

Review the lender's official disclosures and loan documents and ask the lender or servicer about any item you do not understand.

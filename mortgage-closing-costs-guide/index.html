Mortgage Closing Costs Explained

Closing costs are the expenses involved in completing a home purchase or mortgage transaction. They are separate from the down payment and can include lender charges, title and settlement services, government charges, and prepaid or escrow items.

THE BASICS

What are mortgage closing costs?

Closing costs are charges and expenses associated with making and completing a mortgage or real-estate transaction. The exact items vary by loan, property, lender, location, and transaction structure.

Some costs are connected to the mortgage itself, while others are connected to transferring, documenting, protecting, or preparing the property transaction. Some amounts may also be collected in advance for future expenses such as property taxes or homeowners insurance.

Common categories of closing costs

1. Lender and loan costs

These can include origination-related charges, underwriting or processing charges, and other fees associated with obtaining the mortgage. Discount points, if chosen, are another upfront mortgage cost.

2. Third-party services

A transaction can involve services such as an appraisal, credit-related services, title work, settlement services, inspections, or other professional services. Which services appear depends on the transaction.

3. Government and recording charges

Local or state government charges can apply to recording documents, transferring property, or other transaction requirements. These costs vary substantially by location.

4. Prepaid expenses and escrow

Some money collected at closing is not a lender fee. It may cover future property taxes, homeowners insurance, prepaid interest, or initial escrow funding. These amounts depend on the property, closing date, lender requirements, and local rules.

Closing costs vs. down payment

The down payment is the portion of the purchase price you pay toward the home rather than financing with the mortgage. Closing costs are transaction expenses and prepaid amounts associated with completing the purchase and loan.

For example, suppose an illustrative home purchase is $400,000 and the buyer plans an $80,000 down payment. If eligible closing costs and other closing amounts total $8,000, the buyer could need about $88,000 of cash for those two categories before considering credits, deposits already paid, prorations, or other adjustments. This is only an example; actual cash to close can be different.

What does “cash to close” mean?

Cash to close is the amount the buyer needs to provide at closing after the transaction's credits, deposits, adjustments, and other applicable amounts are taken into account. It is not always simply “down payment + closing costs.”

For that reason, buyers should review the transaction documents rather than relying on a percentage or a simple rule of thumb.

Seller credits and lender credits

Depending on the transaction and loan rules, a seller may contribute toward certain buyer closing costs. A lender may also offer credits that reduce certain upfront costs. These credits have conditions and limits, so they should be evaluated against the overall loan terms.

Lender credits can be associated with accepting a higher interest rate, so comparing only the upfront credit can give an incomplete picture. Review the interest rate, monthly payment, upfront costs, and expected time in the loan together.

How to review your Closing Disclosure

The Closing Disclosure is the document used to show the final terms and costs of the mortgage transaction. Compare it with your earlier disclosures and ask the lender or settlement professional about material differences you do not understand.

Questions to ask before closing

  • Which charges are lender fees and which are third-party services?
  • Which amounts are prepaid or going into an escrow account?
  • Have any seller or lender credits been applied?
  • Are discount points included?
  • What is the final amount of cash to close?
  • Which costs can change before closing and why?
  • Are there local taxes, recording fees, or other property-specific charges?

Frequently asked questions

Are closing costs the same for every home buyer?

No. Costs can vary by lender, loan program, property, location, services used, and the terms negotiated in the transaction.

Can closing costs be rolled into a mortgage?

Whether costs can be financed depends on the loan program, lender rules, property value, and transaction structure. Some costs may be financed while others must be paid separately. Confirm the available options with the lender.

Are prepaid taxes and insurance a closing cost?

They can appear among the amounts due at closing, but prepaid and escrow amounts serve a different purpose from many lender or third-party fees. Review the detailed disclosure to see how each amount is categorized.

Does a larger down payment eliminate closing costs?

No. A larger down payment reduces the amount financed, but it does not automatically eliminate the other expenses involved in completing the transaction.

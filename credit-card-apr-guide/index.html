Credit Card APR Explained

Learn what credit card APR means, the types of APR you may see, and how APR can affect the cost of carrying a balance.

Educational finance guidance

APR is an annualized percentage rate disclosed for credit-card financing. A card can have different APRs for different transaction types or circumstances.

Purchase APR

The purchase APR is the rate disclosed for balances from eligible purchases when interest applies under the card agreement.

Balance-transfer APR

A balance-transfer transaction can have a separate APR and may also have a balance-transfer fee. Promotional terms can expire.

Cash-advance APR

Cash advances can have a separate APR and may begin accruing interest under different terms from purchases. Review the agreement before using this feature.

Variable APR

Some credit cards have variable APRs tied to a reference rate or other contractual mechanism. The agreement explains when and how the rate can change.

How APR affects payoff cost

A higher APR can increase interest charges when a balance is carried, which can lengthen repayment if payments remain unchanged.

Illustrative example

Two cards with the same $5,000 balance but different APRs can generate different interest costs. The exact amount depends on the issuer's calculation method, transaction dates and payments.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

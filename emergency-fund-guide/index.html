Emergency Fund Guide: How to Build a Financial Cushion

Learn what an emergency fund is, what expenses it can help cover, where to keep it, and how to build the habit of saving.

Educational finance guidance

An emergency fund is a cash reserve intended for unexpected or financially disruptive expenses. The amount and structure depend on your household's income, expenses and financial circumstances.

What an emergency fund is for

Common uses can include an unexpected repair, medical or household expense, temporary income interruption or other unplanned cost. The purpose is to reduce the need to rely immediately on expensive debt.

How much should you save?

There is no single amount that fits every household. Consider essential monthly expenses, income stability, insurance, dependents, existing savings and access to other resources.

Start with a realistic first target

A smaller initial reserve can be easier to establish than waiting to build a large balance. Regular automatic transfers can help create consistency.

Where to keep the money

Emergency savings are generally kept in an accessible, relatively low-risk account appropriate to the person's needs. The specific account should be evaluated for access, fees, rate and applicable protections.

Illustrative example

A household with $2,500 of essential monthly expenses might first set a modest starter reserve, then gradually increase it as cash flow permits. The target should reflect the household's own risk and obligations.

Frequently asked questions

Will a calculator tell me what I should borrow or spend?

No. Calculators are educational tools that apply the assumptions entered by the user. Actual borrowing terms, eligibility and personal affordability require additional information.

Are loan and credit rules the same for every lender?

No. Lenders can have different product terms, underwriting criteria, fees and disclosures. The lender's agreement and disclosures control an actual transaction.

Where should I verify the terms of an actual account?

Review the lender or card issuer's official disclosures, statements and agreement, and ask the provider about any item you do not understand.

CALCULATION METHOD

How this US cash-out refinance calculator works

A cash-out refinance replaces an existing mortgage with a new mortgage. The new loan can be larger than the existing balance, with the difference used for cash to the borrower and, depending on the transaction, eligible costs or other payoffs.

This calculator first estimates the maximum new loan as home value × target LTV. It then subtracts the existing mortgage balance, refinance closing costs and other liens/debt entered by the user to estimate the maximum cash that could fit within that target.

The requested new loan is calculated as current mortgage balance + requested cash-out + closing costs + other debt paid from the refinance. If that amount exceeds the target maximum loan, the requested scenario is above the entered LTV assumption.

Worked cash-out refinance example

Suppose your home is worth $450,000, your mortgage balance is $260,000, your target maximum LTV is 80%, and you request $50,000 cash with $8,000 in closing costs.

  • Target maximum new loan: $450,000 × 80% = $360,000
  • Loan needed for existing balance + cash + closing costs: $260,000 + $50,000 + $8,000 = $318,000
  • Remaining mathematical room under the target: $360,000 − $260,000 − $8,000 = $92,000
  • The $50,000 requested cash fits within this illustrative LTV assumption.

This example does not establish an actual lender limit. The lender may use a different maximum LTV, property valuation, credit criteria or loan program.

Why closing costs matter

Refinancing has transaction costs. Depending on the loan structure, some costs can be paid at closing or financed into the new loan. Financing costs increases the amount borrowed, which can increase the payment and reduce the equity remaining in the property. The CFPB notes that adding closing costs to the loan amount is one way a refinance can be structured, while lender credits can offset costs in exchange for a higher rate.

Cash-out refinance vs. HELOC

A cash-out refinance replaces the existing mortgage with a new mortgage. A HELOC is generally a separate revolving line of credit secured by the home. The two products can have different rates, payment structures, costs and effects on the existing mortgage. The CFPB describes cash-out refinancing as replacing an existing mortgage with a larger mortgage and taking the difference in cash.

Using cash-out proceeds for other debt

Cash-out proceeds may be used for purposes such as home improvements or debt consolidation, depending on the loan and borrower. The CFPB has noted that converting non-mortgage debt into mortgage debt changes the risk because the new debt is secured by the home.

CASH-OUT REFINANCE GUIDE

How this cash-out refinance calculator works

A cash-out refinance replaces an existing mortgage with a new, larger mortgage. The new loan first pays off the existing mortgage and other entered liens, while the remaining amount can be received as cash, subject to the loan terms and lender requirements.

This calculator estimates the maximum loan supported by your entered home value and target maximum LTV, then compares that with the requested cash-out amount. It also estimates the new principal-and-interest payment using the new rate and term.

Loan-to-value matters

LTV compares the mortgage debt with the property's value. A cash-out transaction increases the new mortgage balance, so the amount of cash available depends on the lender's permitted LTV and the existing debt that must be paid off. Actual limits vary by loan program, property, occupancy, credit profile and lender.

Worked example

For example, suppose a home is worth $400,000, the current mortgage balance is $220,000, the target maximum LTV is 80%, and you request $50,000 cash out. An 80% LTV corresponds to a maximum secured loan of $320,000 before considering closing costs or other liens. The calculator then estimates the resulting payment using your selected new rate and term.

Cash-out refinance vs. HELOC

A cash-out refinance replaces the existing first mortgage, while a HELOC is generally a separate credit line secured by the home. A refinance can change the rate, term and entire mortgage balance; a HELOC generally leaves the existing first mortgage in place. CFPB notes that the relative cost depends on the specific terms and circumstances.

Consider the full cost

Refinancing can involve origination charges, appraisal or title costs, points, government fees and other closing costs. Rolling costs into the new loan can reduce the amount paid upfront but increases the loan balance and can increase interest paid over time.

Important limitations

This calculator does not determine eligibility, appraisal value, lender-specific LTV limits, APR, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance or exact closing costs. A lender's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure provide the transaction-specific figures.

FAQ

US cash-out refinance calculator FAQs

What is a cash-out refinance?

It is a refinance in which the new mortgage is larger than the existing mortgage balance and the borrower receives cash from the transaction after accounting for the existing debt and applicable costs.

How much cash can I get from a cash-out refinance?

It depends on the property's value, the lender's maximum LTV, your existing secured debt, closing costs and underwriting requirements. This calculator shows a mathematical estimate using your selected LTV assumption.

What is the maximum loan-to-value?

It is the maximum percentage of the property's value that the assumed new mortgage can represent. Actual cash-out LTV limits vary by lender, loan program, occupancy, property and borrower.

Do closing costs reduce cash available?

They can. If closing costs are financed within the new loan, they use part of the available loan amount and reduce the cash that can fit under a target LTV.

Can I use cash-out proceeds to pay other debt?

Depending on the loan and borrower, cash-out proceeds can be used for debt consolidation or other purposes. If debt is moved into a mortgage secured by the home, the repayment risk is different because the home serves as collateral.

Why can the new payment be higher?

The new mortgage may have a larger principal balance, a different interest rate or a different term. Financing closing costs or other debt can also increase the amount borrowed.

Does the calculator include taxes and insurance?

No. The displayed monthly payment is principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and other homeownership costs are excluded.

Is this a lender quote or approval calculator?

No. It is an educational estimate. A lender will determine the actual property value, eligible loan amount, rate, costs and qualification.

Can I finance refinance closing costs?

Depending on the transaction, some costs may be financed into the new loan. Doing so increases the balance and can affect payment and equity.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not determine eligibility, appraisal value, lender pricing, tax treatment or an approved cash-out amount. Review the Loan Estimate and other lender disclosures for the actual refinance terms and costs.

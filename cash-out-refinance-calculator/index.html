CASH-OUT REFINANCE GUIDE

How the cash-out refinance estimate works

A cash-out refinance replaces an existing mortgage with a new mortgage. The new loan pays the estimated existing mortgage payoff and any other secured debts you choose to include. If the new loan is large enough, the remaining proceeds can be paid to you at closing after the financed costs and other required amounts are accounted for. The lender determines the actual payoff, eligible loan amount, costs and cash due at closing.

Calculator formulas

This tool uses the home value and your chosen target loan-to-value (LTV) assumption to estimate a target maximum new first-mortgage amount:

  • Target maximum new loan: home value × target LTV percentage.
  • Estimated new loan amount: mortgage payoff + other secured debt payoffs + closing costs financed into the loan + cash requested.
  • Maximum additional cash: target maximum new loan − mortgage payoff − other secured debt payoffs − financed closing costs, with the displayed cash amount floored at $0.
  • New first-mortgage LTV: estimated new loan amount ÷ home value.
  • Estimated remaining equity: home value − estimated new loan amount.

The LTV shown is the new first mortgage divided by the home value you enter. It is not a combined loan-to-value calculation. A lien that remains on the property rather than being paid off may affect a lender's combined-LTV assessment, but this basic calculator does not model that lien.

Worked example

Assume a home value of $450,000, an estimated current mortgage payoff of $260,000, a target LTV of 80%, financed closing costs of $8,000, no other secured-debt payoff, and $50,000 cash requested.

  • Target maximum new loan: $450,000 × 80% = $360,000.
  • Estimated new loan: $260,000 + $8,000 + $50,000 = $318,000.
  • Maximum additional cash under the same target: $360,000 − $260,000 − $8,000 = $92,000.
  • Estimated new first-mortgage LTV: $318,000 ÷ $450,000 = 70.67%.
  • Estimated remaining equity before selling costs or value changes: $450,000 − $318,000 = $132,000.

At an illustrative fixed rate of 6.50% for 30 years, the estimated payment on $318,000 is about $2,009.98 per month for principal and interest, with about $405,591.47 in total scheduled interest if all payments are made as modeled. This is a mathematical example, not a rate offer or approval.

Use a payoff figure, not just the balance on a statement

The amount needed to pay off a mortgage can differ from the principal balance shown on a monthly statement. A lender payoff statement may include interest through a specified date and other amounts due. Enter the best current payoff estimate available to you. The final closing agent and lender use transaction-specific payoff figures.

What the closing-cost input means

Enter only the portion of refinance costs that you expect to finance into the new mortgage. Costs paid separately in cash are not added to the modeled new loan. Actual closing costs can include lender charges and third-party services; compare the Loan Estimate from each lender rather than relying on a generic estimate. The CFPB explains how to review origination charges, other loan costs and lender credits in a Loan Estimate.

Cash-out refinance versus a HELOC

A cash-out refinance replaces the existing first mortgage with a new mortgage. A home equity line of credit (HELOC) is typically a separate credit line secured by the property and may leave the existing first mortgage in place. They can differ in interest rate, fees, repayment structure and total borrowing cost. Compare the full terms rather than choosing by the initial payment alone.

Important risks and limitations

Using mortgage debt to pay off credit cards, auto loans or other non-mortgage debt changes the risk: the new debt is secured by your home. If payments become unaffordable, you could put the property at risk. A lower monthly payment does not necessarily mean a lower total cost, especially when debt is spread over a longer term. Consider the total interest, fees, the new term and alternatives before borrowing against home equity. The CFPB discusses these trade-offs in its research on cash-out refinancing and non-mortgage debt paydown.

FAQ

Cash-out refinance calculator FAQs

How much cash can I take out?

It depends on the lender's permitted loan amount, the property's accepted value, the mortgage payoff, other liens, financed costs and underwriting. This calculator gives a mathematical estimate using the target LTV you select; it does not establish a lender's limit.

Why does the calculator ask for a mortgage payoff?

The refinance must account for the amount required to satisfy the existing mortgage. That payoff can differ from the unpaid principal balance because interest and other amounts may be due through the closing date.

Are closing costs deducted from cash-out?

Costs entered as financed into the new loan use part of the target loan amount and reduce the additional cash that fits under the selected LTV. Costs paid separately are not included in this loan-amount calculation.

Does 80% LTV apply to every cash-out refinance?

No. The target LTV is an assumption for scenario planning. Actual limits depend on loan program, property, occupancy, borrower and lender requirements.

Does the monthly payment include taxes and insurance?

No. The displayed payment is principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues and escrow items are excluded.

Is a cash-out refinance the same as a HELOC?

No. A cash-out refinance replaces the existing first mortgage. A HELOC is generally a separate credit line secured by the home and may leave the existing first mortgage unchanged.

Is this a lender quote?

No. It is an educational estimate based on the numbers you enter. Only a lender can provide transaction-specific terms and determine whether you qualify.

Educational estimate: This calculator does not determine eligibility, appraisal value, lender pricing, tax treatment or an approved cash-out amount. Review the Loan Estimate and other lender disclosures for the actual refinance terms and costs.

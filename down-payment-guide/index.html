DOWN PAYMENT BASICS

What is a down payment?

A down payment is the portion of a home's purchase price that the buyer pays upfront rather than financing through the mortgage. The remaining eligible amount is generally financed with a mortgage, subject to the loan program and lender requirements.

For a hypothetical $400,000 home, a 10% down payment would be $40,000, leaving $360,000 to finance before considering other transaction adjustments. This example is illustrative.

Is 20% down required to buy a house?

No single down-payment percentage applies to every US home purchase. Minimum requirements can vary by loan program, lender, property type, occupancy and borrower circumstances.

Some conventional borrowers may qualify with a down payment below 20%. Government-backed programs can have different requirements. A larger down payment can reduce the amount borrowed, but it also requires more cash upfront.

How does a down payment affect your mortgage?

For the same purchase price, a larger down payment generally means a smaller starting loan balance. A smaller balance can reduce the scheduled principal-and-interest payment and the amount of interest charged over the life of the loan, assuming the other loan terms are unchanged.

A down payment can also affect loan-to-value ratio and, for some conventional loans, whether borrower-paid PMI applies.

Down payment and PMI

With many conventional mortgages, a down payment below 20% can result in PMI. The actual requirement and cost depend on the loan and lender.

Putting more money down can potentially reduce or eliminate borrower-paid PMI, but using additional cash for the down payment also reduces the cash available for reserves and other needs. Compare the full scenario rather than focusing only on PMI.

Down payment vs. closing costs

The down payment is applied toward the purchase price. Closing costs are separate transaction expenses that can include lender, title, settlement, government and prepaid or escrow-related items.

For that reason, a buyer who plans to make a $40,000 down payment on a $400,000 home should not assume $40,000 is the total cash required at closing.

How much cash should you keep after buying?

The amount of cash to keep available after closing depends on personal circumstances and is not a universal percentage. Buyers may need to account for emergency savings, moving costs, repairs, maintenance, insurance deductibles and other obligations.

Using every available dollar for a larger down payment may change the mortgage payment, but it can also leave less liquidity after the purchase. Consider the complete cash-flow picture before choosing a down payment.

How interest rate and loan term fit into the decision

The down payment should be evaluated together with the mortgage rate and term. A smaller loan at one rate and term can have a different payment and total interest cost from a larger loan at another rate or term.

Worked example

Imagine a hypothetical $350,000 purchase. At 10% down, the down payment would be $35,000 and the starting mortgage amount would be approximately $315,000 before other adjustments. At 20% down, the down payment would be $70,000 and the starting mortgage would be approximately $280,000.

The 20% scenario requires another $35,000 upfront but starts with a $35,000 smaller mortgage. Whether that trade-off makes sense depends on the buyer's cash reserves, loan terms, PMI requirements and other financial considerations.

Questions to ask before choosing a down payment

  • What is the minimum down payment for the loan program?
  • Will PMI or another mortgage-insurance cost apply?
  • How much cash is needed for closing costs and prepaid items?
  • How much money will remain available after closing?
  • How does the down payment change the monthly mortgage payment?
  • How does it change the total interest under the quoted loan terms?
  • Are there lender or program-specific requirements that affect the choice?

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a house with less than 20% down?

Yes, some borrowers qualify for mortgages with less than 20% down. The minimum depends on the loan program, lender, property and borrower circumstances.

Does a larger down payment always mean a lower monthly payment?

For the same purchase price, rate and term, a larger down payment generally produces a smaller loan balance and therefore a lower principal-and-interest payment. Other costs can still vary.

Is the down payment part of closing costs?

No. The down payment is a separate part of the purchase transaction, although both the down payment and closing costs can contribute to the total cash needed at closing.

Does a 20% down payment always eliminate mortgage insurance?

Not necessarily for every mortgage structure. Conventional PMI rules differ from mortgage-insurance requirements for other loan programs. Confirm the specific loan terms.

Should I use all my savings for a down payment?

There is no universal answer. Compare the mortgage benefits of a larger down payment with the importance of retaining cash for emergencies, repairs, moving costs and other obligations.

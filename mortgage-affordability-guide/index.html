US HOME-BUYING GUIDE

How to Estimate Mortgage Affordability

A practical US guide to estimating a comfortable mortgage range using income, recurring debts, down payment, interest rate, property taxes, insurance and mortgage insurance.

What does mortgage affordability mean?

Mortgage affordability is an estimate of the home price and housing payment that may fit within a household's finances. A lender's qualification decision and a household's own budget are not necessarily the same thing.

Use the Mortgage Affordability Calculator to test income, recurring debt and housing-cost assumptions. Then use the Mortgage Calculator to examine the payment for a specific loan amount.

Income is only one part of the calculation

A useful affordability analysis starts with gross monthly income but also considers recurring debt obligations and housing expenses. Debt-to-income ratios are commonly used in mortgage underwriting, but the applicable requirements vary by loan program, lender and borrower circumstances.

Other household expenses—such as food, utilities, transportation, childcare, healthcare, savings and existing financial commitments—can affect what payment is sustainable even when a borrower meets a lender's underwriting requirements.

Housing costs to include

Don't look only at principal and interest. A mortgage payment can also involve property taxes, homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance. HOA dues and other property-specific costs may need to be considered separately.

How debt-to-income ratios fit in

Debt-to-income ratio, or DTI, compares recurring monthly debt obligations with gross monthly income. Mortgage underwriting can consider housing expense ratios and total DTI, but the precise thresholds depend on the loan program and lender.

For example, a household earning $8,000 in gross monthly income with $1,200 of recurring non-housing debt has a different affordability profile from a household earning the same income with $2,500 of recurring debt.

DTI is a screening measure, not a complete household budget. A borrower should also consider cash reserves, emergency savings and expected homeownership costs.

Down payment matters

A down payment reduces the starting loan balance. It can also affect loan-to-value ratio and, depending on the loan, whether mortgage insurance applies.

For example, on a $400,000 home, a $40,000 down payment represents 10% of the purchase price and leaves a starting principal of $360,000 before other financing adjustments. Use the Down Payment Calculator to compare cash requirements.

Interest rate and loan term

The interest rate directly affects the scheduled principal-and-interest payment. The loan term determines how many monthly payments are scheduled. A longer term can produce a lower scheduled payment while increasing the total interest paid when the loan is carried to maturity.

Because mortgage rates change over time, affordability estimates should be tested across more than one rate assumption rather than treated as a permanent number.

Taxes, insurance and PMI can change the result

Two homes with the same purchase price can have different monthly housing costs because property-tax rates, insurance premiums, HOA dues and mortgage-insurance requirements differ.

This is why a home-price estimate should be converted into a complete monthly housing-cost estimate before a buyer decides whether the payment fits the household budget.

Affordability example

Consider a household with $8,000 in gross monthly income, $1,200 in recurring monthly debt, and a planned $60,000 down payment. Test several interest rates and home prices rather than treating one output as a recommendation. Add estimated taxes, insurance and PMI to understand the broader monthly housing cost.

The Mortgage Affordability Calculator is designed for this scenario testing. Its result is an estimate, not a lender preapproval or underwriting decision.

Affordability vs. preapproval

A calculator can help with planning, but it cannot verify income, assets, credit history, employment, property details or lender-specific underwriting requirements. A mortgage preapproval is based on information reviewed by a lender and may be subject to conditions.

Questions to ask before choosing a home price

  • How much cash will remain after the down payment and closing costs?
  • Can the household continue saving after the projected housing payment?
  • What happens to the budget if taxes or insurance increase?
  • Is there room for repairs and maintenance?
  • Does the payment still work under a higher interest-rate assumption?

Mortgage affordability FAQ

How much house can I afford?

There is no single percentage that works for every household. Income, recurring debts, loan terms, taxes, insurance, down payment, reserves and other expenses all affect affordability.

What is DTI?

DTI is debt-to-income ratio. It compares recurring monthly debt obligations with gross monthly income and is one factor that can be used in mortgage underwriting.

Does a bigger down payment increase affordability?

It can reduce the amount borrowed and may reduce the monthly payment. It can also change loan-to-value and mortgage-insurance requirements. However, using too much cash for a down payment can reduce emergency reserves.

Does a mortgage calculator tell me what a lender will approve?

No. A calculator is a planning tool. A lender evaluates borrower and property information under its applicable underwriting requirements.

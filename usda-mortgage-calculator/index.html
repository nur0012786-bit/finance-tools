CALCULATION METHOD

How this USDA mortgage calculator works

This tool models a USDA Section 502 Guaranteed Loan as a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage. It estimates principal and interest, the upfront guarantee fee, the first-year annual fee, property taxes, homeowners insurance and optional homeowners association (HOA) dues.

1. Base loan and upfront guarantee fee

The base loan starts with the home price minus the down payment entered. For the default option that finances the full upfront guarantee fee, the calculator grosses up the base loan so the fee equals the stated percentage of the final loan amount. At a 1% fee, the calculation is: base loan ÷ 0.99 = total loan including the financed fee. If you uncheck the financing option, the fee is estimated as a cash amount due at closing and is not added to the principal balance.

2. Monthly principal and interest

Principal and interest use the standard fixed-rate mortgage payment formula with a 360-month term. The interest-rate field is an assumption you supply; USDA does not set the rate offered by a lender. Use the rate in your Loan Estimate or lender quote rather than treating the prefilled example rate as a current market rate.

3. First-year USDA annual-fee estimate

USDA's annual fee is based on the average scheduled unpaid principal balance, not simply the original loan balance for every month. This calculator averages the scheduled beginning balance for payments 1 through 12, applies the annual-fee percentage and divides by 12 to show a first-year monthly escrow equivalent. Actual servicing and escrow amounts are determined by the lender and USDA methodology; later annual fees can change as the mortgage amortizes.

For USDA's published calculation approach, review the Guaranteed Annual Fee Calculation Methodology and the USDA Guaranteed Underwriting System (GUS) Lender User Guide.

4. Taxes, insurance and HOA dues

For a quick estimate, the property-tax percentage is applied to the home purchase price and divided by 12. Actual property taxes are based on local assessment and tax rules, which may not equal the purchase price. Homeowners insurance is the annual premium you enter divided by 12. HOA dues are included only when you enter them.

USDA PROGRAM FEES

USDA guarantee fees for fiscal year 2027

USDA Rural Development's September 16, 2026 notice states that, for fiscal year 2027 (October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027), the Single-Family Housing Guaranteed Loan Program applies a 1.00% upfront guarantee fee and a 0.35% annual fee to purchase and refinance transactions.

The upfront fee may be paid at closing or financed in whole or in part, subject to USDA program rules and the transaction's loan-to-value limits. When the entire fee is financed, the total loan is calculated by dividing the base loan amount by one minus the fee rate. For example, a $100,000 base loan with a 1% fully financed fee produces a $101,010.10 total loan and a $1,010.10 fee.

The annual fee is based on the average annual scheduled unpaid principal balance. It is not the same as conventional private mortgage insurance (PMI), and the first-year monthly amount is an estimate for payment planning. Confirm the applicable fee structure with an approved lender and check USDA notices for future fiscal years.

ELIGIBILITY AND PROGRAM RULES

Who may qualify for a USDA home loan?

This calculator estimates payment amounts. It does not decide whether a borrower, household or property qualifies for the USDA Section 502 Guaranteed Loan Program.

USDA eligibility includes household-income limits, an eligible property location, owner occupancy as a primary residence and applicable citizenship or qualified-alien requirements. Applicants apply through an approved lender, which reviews the loan under USDA rules and its own underwriting standards.

USDA's eligibility website lets you check a property address and review income eligibility by area. Eligibility can vary with location and household circumstances, so use the official tools rather than relying on a general statement that a home is “rural.”

Check USDA property and income eligibility →

USDA's Section 502 Guaranteed Loan Program can offer 100% financing to eligible borrowers. That means a qualifying borrower may not need a down payment; it does not mean the purchase has no closing costs, prepaid expenses, inspections, moving costs or other cash requirements. Ask an approved lender which costs may be financed or covered in your specific transaction.

The program is not limited to first-time home buyers. USDA's program page states that its guaranteed loans are offered at a 30-year fixed rate only. Rates are determined by individual lenders, so compare written offers.

COMMON QUESTIONS

USDA mortgage calculator FAQs

Can a USDA loan have no down payment?

Eligible borrowers may qualify for 100% financing under the Section 502 Guaranteed Loan Program. Income, property, occupancy, citizenship or qualified-status and lender requirements still apply.

What are the USDA guarantee fees for FY 2027?

USDA's September 16, 2026 FY 2027 notice lists a 1.00% upfront guarantee fee and a 0.35% annual fee for purchase and refinance transactions. Fees can change in a future fiscal year.

How is the upfront USDA guarantee fee calculated?

The fee is a percentage of the loan amount. When the entire fee is financed, the total loan amount must include the fee itself. For a 1% fee, the total is the base loan divided by 0.99. The fee can also be paid at closing, subject to the program rules.

How is the USDA annual fee calculated?

USDA bases the annual fee on the average annual scheduled unpaid principal balance. This calculator estimates the first-year monthly escrow equivalent using the average scheduled beginning balance for the first 12 payments. The actual amount is determined through USDA and lender servicing calculations.

Is the USDA annual fee the same as PMI?

No. USDA Guaranteed Loans have an upfront guarantee fee and an annual fee. These are program fees and are different from private mortgage insurance on a conventional mortgage.

Are USDA loans always 30-year fixed?

USDA Rural Development states that Section 502 Guaranteed Loans are offered at a 30-year fixed rate. This calculator uses that term.

Does a USDA loan require a rural property?

The property must be in an area that USDA determines eligible. Use the official USDA eligibility website to check the address; a town's name or population alone is not enough to determine eligibility.

Does 0% down mean no money is needed at closing?

No. No-down-payment financing does not automatically eliminate closing costs, prepaid taxes and insurance, inspections or other expenses. Ask the lender what may be included in the transaction and what cash will be needed.

Does this calculator provide an approval decision or lender quote?

No. It is an educational estimate. It does not evaluate income, debts, credit, property eligibility, lender overlays or underwriting, and it does not provide a loan offer.

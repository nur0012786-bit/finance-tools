LOAN PAYMENT GUIDE

How to use this US loan payment calculator

Enter the amount borrowed, annual interest rate and repayment term. The calculator estimates the regular monthly principal-and-interest payment for a fixed-rate installment loan. It then shows the total interest and total of the scheduled payments over the term.

Enter an optional extra monthly principal payment to compare the regular schedule with an accelerated payoff. The amortization table shows how each payment is divided between principal and interest and how the balance changes after each month.

How a fixed-rate loan payment is calculated

For a loan with equal monthly payments, the standard amortization formula is:

Monthly payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

  • P is the starting principal, or amount borrowed.
  • r is the monthly interest rate, calculated as the annual interest rate divided by 12 and then by 100.
  • n is the total number of monthly payments (loan term in years × 12).

When the interest rate is 0%, the payment is the principal divided evenly by the number of payments. For a loan with interest, each scheduled payment covers the interest accrued for that period and reduces some principal. As the outstanding balance declines, the interest portion generally gets smaller.

Worked example: $25,000 loan at 7% for five years

Assume you borrow $25,000 at a fixed 7% annual interest rate and repay it in 60 equal monthly payments. With no additional fees, the estimated monthly principal-and-interest payment is $495.03.

What an amortization schedule shows

An amortization schedule breaks each payment into interest and principal. Early in the term, more of a fixed payment typically goes toward interest because the balance is higher. As principal is repaid, the interest portion declines and more of each scheduled payment reduces the balance. The remaining-balance column shows the modeled amount still owed after each payment.

Interest rate vs. APR

The interest rate measures the cost of borrowing the principal. APR is a broader measure that can include the interest rate and certain loan fees. This calculator uses the interest rate for the monthly amortization formula; it does not calculate APR or add origination and other lender charges. When comparing offers, review both the rate and APR and compare fees using the lender's written disclosures. Read the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's explanation of interest rate vs. APR.

How extra payments can reduce interest

Interest on a typical amortizing loan is calculated using the outstanding principal balance. An additional payment applied to principal can reduce that balance earlier, which may lower future interest and shorten the payoff period. The savings estimate here assumes the extra amount is paid every month and applied directly to principal. Some lenders require a particular instruction or payment channel for extra principal payments. Check your agreement for payment-allocation rules and prepayment terms.

Fees and the full cost of borrowing

A principal-and-interest calculation is not necessarily the full cost of a loan. Depending on the loan product and lender, charges may include origination or documentation fees, optional credit insurance, late fees or other costs. Fees may be paid upfront, deducted from proceeds or included in the amount financed. Review the loan disclosure and agreement rather than comparing monthly payments alone. The CFPB explains common fees on personal installment loans.

Compare loan offers using the same amount and term. Review the APR, payment, total of payments, fees, repayment flexibility and whether a rate is fixed or variable. A longer term may lower the required payment while increasing the total interest paid.

What this calculator does not model

This tool is designed for a standard fixed-rate installment loan with regular monthly payments. It is not a universal calculator for every borrowing product. It does not model a variable-rate loan, revolving credit-card balance, interest-only period, balloon payment, mortgage escrow, lender-specific daily-interest convention or loan fees and insurance. Use a product-specific calculator for mortgages, auto loans or credit-card balances.

Loan payment calculator FAQs

What information do I need to calculate a loan payment?

You need the amount borrowed, annual interest rate and repayment term. You can also enter an optional extra monthly principal payment to see an accelerated payoff estimate.

Does this calculator use APR or the interest rate?

It uses the annual interest rate as the input to the amortization formula. It does not calculate APR or include lender fees. Use the APR and written disclosures when comparing credit offers.

Does paying extra every month save interest?

It can when the lender applies the extra amount to principal. A lower balance can reduce future interest and shorten the payoff period. Check the contract for payment-allocation instructions and prepayment terms.

What is an amortization schedule?

It is a month-by-month breakdown of the payment, principal repaid, interest charged and remaining loan balance.

Why does a longer loan term usually cost more?

A longer term spreads repayment over more months. The required monthly payment is often lower, but interest can accumulate for longer, increasing the total borrowing cost.

Does this estimate include origination fees or taxes?

No. The calculation models principal and interest only. Any lender fees, taxes, insurance or other charges need to be reviewed separately in the loan documents.

Can I use this for a mortgage or car loan?

The formula is a simplified fixed-rate amortization model, but mortgages and auto loans can have product-specific fees, taxes, insurance and other rules. Use the dedicated Mortgage Calculator or Auto Loan Calculator for those estimates.

LOAN CALCULATOR GUIDE

How this US loan calculator works

The calculator uses standard amortization mathematics for a fixed-rate installment loan. It uses the loan amount, monthly interest rate and number of scheduled monthly payments to estimate the regular payment. Total interest is calculated from the interest accrued as the balance declines.

The model assumes a constant rate and regular monthly payments. It does not include lender-specific fees, insurance, taxes or other charges unless they are already reflected in the amount you enter.

Interest rate vs. APR

The interest rate measures the cost of borrowing. APR can include the interest rate plus certain fees, so it can provide a broader measure when comparing credit offers. The CFPB explains that an interest rate and APR are different measures.

How extra payments change the schedule

If an extra payment is applied to principal, the balance can decline faster. That can reduce future interest and shorten the payoff period. This calculator assumes the extra amount is applied directly to principal each month; your lender or loan agreement determines how additional payments are actually applied.

Loan payment example

For example, enter a $25,000 loan at 7% annual interest for 5 years. Then enter $100 as the extra monthly payment. The calculator will compare the standard schedule with the accelerated schedule and show estimated time and interest savings.

Fees can change the true borrowing cost

A payment calculation based only on principal, interest and term does not necessarily represent the full cost of borrowing. Depending on the loan, lenders may charge origination, documentation, late or other fees. Review the lender disclosure and loan agreement before comparing offers.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What information do I need to calculate a loan payment?

You generally need the principal or loan amount, annual interest rate and repayment term. This calculator also lets you enter an optional extra monthly payment.

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate measures borrowing cost, while APR can incorporate the interest rate plus certain fees. They should not be treated as interchangeable when comparing loan offers.

Does paying extra every month save interest?

It can. When an extra payment is applied to principal, the balance falls faster, which can reduce the amount of interest charged over time. Your lender's payment rules and any prepayment terms should be checked first.

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule shows each payment and how it is divided between interest and principal, along with the remaining loan balance after the payment.

Is the loan calculator accurate?

It provides an estimate based on the values you enter and standard amortization mathematics. Real loan payments may also include fees, taxes, insurance or other charges that are not included here.

Can I use this calculator for different loan amounts?

Yes. Enter the amount, interest rate, term and optional extra payment, then select Calculate loan to generate a new estimate and amortization schedule.

Can an extra payment shorten the loan term?

Yes, if the additional amount is applied to principal, it can reduce the remaining balance faster and potentially shorten the repayment period.

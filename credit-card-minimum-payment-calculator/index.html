How the Credit Card Minimum Payment Calculator Works

Enter your card balance, APR, and an assumed minimum-payment percentage. The calculator estimates the initial minimum payment and models how the balance changes over time. You can then compare it with a higher fixed monthly payment.

Why Minimum Payments Can Extend Repayment

Credit card interest can consume part of a payment before the remaining amount reduces the balance. When the payment is small relative to the balance and APR, principal can decline slowly. The exact result depends on the card agreement and payment rules.

Example

For example, a $5,000 balance at 24% APR with a 2% starting minimum-payment assumption produces a very different repayment pattern from paying a fixed $200 each month. Use the calculator to see the modeled difference rather than relying on a generic payoff estimate.

What Can Change Your Actual Minimum Payment?

  • Your issuer's minimum-payment formula.
  • Interest accrued during the billing cycle.
  • Late fees and other account fees.
  • New purchases or cash advances.
  • Promotional APRs or balance-transfer terms.
  • A fixed minimum-payment floor required by the issuer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a credit card minimum payment?

It is the smallest payment your issuer requires for a billing cycle under your account terms.

Why can paying only the minimum take so long?

Interest can consume part of each payment, leaving less available to reduce the balance.

Does this use my issuer's exact formula?

No. It uses your entered percentage and a simplified monthly-interest model.

Can I compare a higher payment?

Yes. Enter a fixed higher payment to compare modeled payoff time and interest.

Does a lower APR always mean a lower total cost?

A lower APR generally reduces interest under otherwise similar conditions, but fees and payment behavior also matter.

Is the payoff date exact?

No. Treat it as an estimate because actual card calculations and account activity can differ.
